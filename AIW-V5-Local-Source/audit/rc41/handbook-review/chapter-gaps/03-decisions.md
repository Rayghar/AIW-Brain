# Chapter 3 · Decisions

Part of the AIW handbook gap review (AIW-HANDBOOK-GAP-REVIEW-2026-10-30.md). Gaps verified against the AIW source (paths relative to AIW-V5-Local-Source) and challenged by a skeptic; handbook references are "Handbook › heading (section id)".


**How AIW compares with the handbooks.** AIW's Chapter 3 already follows much of the handbooks' decision discipline. It requires at least two fully described alternatives and needs a per-driver effect with reason and evidence for the working choice before acceptance. Its deterministic reading rules out hard-constraint breaks before any weighing, reports dominance and firmness, and never chooses. Architect acceptance, external governance evidence and Sol or rule proposals stay strictly separate. The gaps are in the shape of the record around that comparison. The handbooks' templates start with diagnosis (symptoms, competing explanations, distinguishing evidence). They state each option conditionally (select when, disqualifier, evidence needed) and treat feasibility as a separate three-state gate over mandatory conditions scoped to the decision. They close with a validation plan, reversibility and commitment points, unresolved obligations, a switching condition for the credible alternative and observable review triggers. AIW holds most of these as free text or not at all. An AIW decision must also end in exactly one chosen alternative, so infeasibility, deferral, bounded pilots and composed outcomes cannot be recorded. Each alternative's impact is limited to Chapter 4 responsibilities and existing components, so infrastructure decisions and cross-layer compositions cannot be decided cleanly in Chapter 3. AIW already has the right mechanisms in narrower places: typed applicability checks in guided comparisons, governed architecture units with requires/excludes and obligations, typed Chapter 11 verification actions, Workbench cost comparisons and next-review warnings. Most gaps can therefore be closed by extending these existing paths rather than adding parallel authority paths.

**What AIW already does well**

- **Deterministic decision reading that never chooses** — public/decision-reading.js:1-36: an alternative that breaks a hard constraint is ruled out; dominance is robust over unstated effects; firmness levels 1-3 are exact over every priority-consistent weighting; unstated effects get worst/best ranges; the reading imports no knowledge module and 'never chooses'.
- **Evidence required for the working choice before acceptance** — public/decisions-domain.js:126 raises an error unless every decision driver has a known effect, a reason and evidence on the chosen alternative; public/decisions-domain.js:225 refuses architect acceptance while any error remains; :139 'Architect acceptance does not constitute governance approval'.
- **Revisioned decisions with source fingerprints** — public/decisions-domain.js:68-70, :122: decisions fingerprint linked requirements, groups and drivers; a change raises source-change and voids currency and governance. Any edit creates a new revision with history (decisions-domain.js:156-157).
- **LLM and rule proposals are labelled and cannot decide** — public/decisions-domain.js:40-61: Sol's recommendation is kept but read by no reader; Sol and rule-drafted effects are marked until a person resends them. authorship.js:39 lists decision.record, decision.accept, decision.governance and decision.supersede as person-only review commands.
- **Guided comparisons with typed applicability checks** — public/architecture-knowledge.js:63-64, :140-168: options carry 'requires' fact predicates, and checks are known/blocked/unknown; public/architecture-design.js:95 refuses the preview until every option is assessed; every option is written as a Chapter 3 alternative with rejection reasons (architecture-design.js:108-118).
- **Conflict and contradiction records pinned to revisions** — public/decisions-domain.js:103-105, :141-142: a recorded conflict keeps both revisions and reopens when either changes. Two choices on the same topic with different strategies on shared responsibilities raise an error until resolved.
- **Transparent trade-off analysis in the SDD** — public/sdd-architecture.js:777-812 prints criteria with weights (hard or soft), totals with ranges, effects, standing, result, firmness and sensitivity. The log labels AIW's lean as 'not a choice' (sdd-architecture.js:681-682).
- **Two-way change tracking and governed topic extension** — public/decision-impact.js:1-9 asks every upstream and downstream record a decision reaches to review its change. public/domain-knowledge.js:1-24 lets a released design-concern claim add a decision topic with criteria, options and consequences without code.

### C3-M2 · Several drivers that measure the same outcome are each counted in the decision reading (double counting), with no check  
*Severity low · effort S*

- **Handbooks:** Audit any score for defects. Double-counting several proxies for the same outcome silently gives that outcome extra weight. Test for it, as for ordinal relabelling and unknowns given neutral scores.  
  _Refs:_ Quality Architecture Vol 9 › QAE-DP-006 Universal architecture scores without defensible assumptions (Quality_Architecture_Volume_9_Handbook__03)
- **AIW today:** weighOptions adds weight × effect for every driver a decision names, so two drivers on the same concern (for example two latency scenarios for one path) each pull the total. The firmness levels test every priority-consistent weighting, which covers ordinal relabelling of priorities but not duplicated criteria. Chapter 2 flags possible duplicates only when two drivers share a title. Nothing checks shared quality category and metric, or shared source requirements with identical effects.  
  _Evidence:_ public/decision-reading.js:16-17, :35, :52-60 — totals sum each named criterion; public/decision-reading.js:19-25 — firmness over priority-consistent weightings; public/quality-domain.js:173, :187 — duplicate driver check by identical title only
- **Why it matters:** How a concern is split into drivers can decide the lead. The firmness level then reports a robust lead that is an artefact of the driver cut, and reviewers cannot see it.
- **Recommendation:** Add a reading line and a Validate warning 'possible double count' when two drivers a decision names share a quality category and metric, or link the same requirements and carry identical effects on every alternative. Print a second reading line with them counted once at the higher priority, without changing the recorded totals. A person may mark them 'distinct outcomes' with a reason on the decision. The SDD trade-off analysis prints the warning. Nothing is merged automatically.

### C3-M4 · Governance records no independent-reader usefulness check (driving condition, rejected alternative, reconsideration trigger, supporting evidence)  
*Severity low · effort S*

- **Handbooks:** Test a decision record's usefulness by asking a reviewer who did not write it four questions: what condition drove the choice, what alternative was rejected, what event would trigger reconsideration and what evidence supports the claim. If the reviewer cannot answer, improve the record.  
  _Refs:_ Software Application Design Handbook › SAD 04 › Verify decision usefulness (Software_Application_Design_Handbook__01); Software Design Component Dictionary Master › SCD-CH01 › Verify decision usefulness (Software_Design_Component_Dictionary_Master__01)
- **AIW today:** decision.governance holds an outcome, a free-text reviewer and free-text evidence; the reviewer and evidence need only be non-empty. Nothing checks that the reviewer differs from the decision owner, and no answers to the four questions are recorded. Mechanical consistency checks exist (broken links, open fields).  
  _Evidence:_ public/record-fields.js:132 — governance: status, reviewer, evidence; public/decisions-domain.js:230 — reviewer and evidence need only be non-empty; public/decisions-domain.js:121 — broken-link consistency check
- **Why it matters:** Approval evidence can be recorded on a decision whose trigger, rejected option or supporting evidence a second reader cannot find. The approval then certifies the record's existence, not its usefulness.
- **Recommendation:** Add an optional usefulness block to decision.governance, recorded by a reviewer named differently from the decision owner. The reviewer records the four answers, or 'could not answer' for any of them. A 'could not answer' makes 'approved' unavailable and points to the field to complete (C3-06, C3-10, C3-09). It stays a person's review record; Sol and knowledge never fill it. Reviewer independence and remit remain with the enterprise-alignment governance work.
- **Depends on:** C3-06, C3-10, deep-dive enterprise-alignment: Governance is one record per baseline; there is no design-authority workflow and conditions are not tracked
