# Chapter 9 · Security

Part of the AIW handbook gap review (AIW-HANDBOOK-GAP-REVIEW-2026-10-30.md). Gaps verified against the AIW source (paths relative to AIW-V5-Local-Source) and challenged by a skeptic; handbook references are "Handbook › heading (section id)".


**How AIW compares with the handbooks.** Chapter 9 follows the handbooks' core chain well. Each threat targets real design objects, a control is justified by a threat, evidence is stamped and goes stale when the control changes, a risk treatment is kept separate from governance, and Chapter 10 binds every control to a runtime plan. The handbooks also prescribe the analysis that turns this chain into a defensible security design, and that analysis is missing. They inventory every route to a protected effect and check that each route is covered. They follow an authorisation decision through time (issuer, decision point, enforcement point, re-check moment, cache and revocation window). They state the tenancy and isolation model, give each control a role (prevent, detect, respond or recover) and layer, and analyse common cause and escalation paths. They also derive security obligations from every pattern, mechanism, product and topology chosen elsewhere. AIW records most of these points as prose (control enforcement and mechanism, contract authorization, path access, Chapter 7 accessPlan). Its checks treat coverage as one control per affected object, so a detective control, or a control on the data with an unprotected export route, clears a threat. Many reader items repeat gaps that the deep-dives already cover, so they are cited as dependencies here, not repeated: the identity and access model, secret and key lifecycle, the obligation register, evidence class and negative tests, privacy and purpose, the control catalogue and picker, and the SDD security section. The new gaps below are the analytic method steps the dives did not name. A few reader items are not raised as separate gaps because a dive already covers them or they are niche: differential-privacy accounting, attestation selection, purpose-limited partner evidence sharing, typed configuration layers, and TLS termination per segment.

### C9-01 · Protection coverage is judged per object, not per access route: no inventory of routes to a protected effect or sensitive data, and no check that each route crosses an enforcing control  
*Severity high · effort L*

- **Handbooks:** Inventory every route to a protected effect or sensitive value: interactive API, bulk and batch, scheduled jobs, export and download, admin and support tools, direct store queries, backup and restore, logs, caches, async consumers, retrieval, alternate origins and listeners. Show that each route crosses a non-bypassable enforcement point that gives the same denial. Measure coverage over the whole inventory with declared exclusions, and treat new routes as untested until assessed. Reachability is analysed transitively (QA → shared services → production) and per address family. Masking, a WAF or a private network never stands in for authorisation on a route.

### C9-02 · No authorisation decision chain or timing per protected operation: issuer and audience, decision and enforcement points, when authority is re-checked, decision caching, revocation window, policy-outage behaviour  
*Severity high · effort L*

Decide whether authority is checked at submission, execution, retrieval or acceptance. Redrive and replay into an effect need present-day authority. ### C9-04 · Controls have no preventive, detective or corrective role, so coverage counts detection as prevention and a threat has no prevent/detect/respond/recover structure  
*Severity medium · effort M*

- **Handbooks:** Place controls on the failure path from initiating condition to harm, at the last reversible intervention point, and give each a preventive, detective or corrective role. A detective control needs a detection window, false-positive tolerance, response capacity and correction authority. Each material threat gets prevention, detection, containment and recovery, with residual risk stated. Coverage is a matrix of path classes × prevent/detect/recover (verified, failed, not exercised, not applicable). Detection never counts as prevention, and a failed hard boundary is not offset by an aggregate percentage. Each control has an owner, a trigger, an exception path, a review interval and an operating burden, and is tested by constructing the violation it should catch.

- **AIW today:** A control has a category and a failureMode. The failureMode values (Deny or contain, Detect and respond, Explicit exception) describe posture when the control is unavailable, not its role. A search of public/*.js finds no preventive, detective or corrective concept. 'uncovered' and the Model coverage accept any linked control that names the object, so a single Detection & audit control clears an 'Unauthorised action' threat. The walkthrough counts any complete control as usable. Controls have no trigger, detection window, response capacity, review interval or operating-burden field; only risk treatments have a next-review date.

- **Why it matters:** Defence that only detects is the handbooks' 'control counts substituted for threat coverage' failure. AIW turns such a threat green and prints it as covered. Reviewers cannot see whether a Critical threat is prevented, only detected, or recoverable.
