# Chapter 1 · Requirements

Part of the AIW handbook gap review (AIW-HANDBOOK-GAP-REVIEW-2026-10-30.md). Gaps verified against the AIW source (paths relative to AIW-V5-Local-Source) and challenged by a skeptic; handbook references are "Handbook › heading (section id)".


**How AIW compares with the handbooks.** Chapter 1 has the right shape: a brief, then context, journey and requirements. Rules check testability, every requirement must link to an outcome and to a journey step, provenance and open questions are handled with discipline, the BPMN process gets structural checks, and relationships are typed against ArchiMate 3.1. The handbooks, though, treat this stage as the place where the business contract is fixed as data before any mechanism is chosen. That contract covers customer-operation promises with success boundaries and recovery obligations, journey terminal outcomes with closure predicates and an explicit unknown state, designed exception paths, and constraint predicates with a change authority and the harm they prevent. It also covers decision tables with hit policy and unknown-input semantics, versioned outcome definitions, and typed accountability. AIW keeps most of this as free text, or pushes it down to Chapter 8 execution agreements, which are per contract and arrive late. Validate checks wording and linkage. It does not check whether the business logic is complete: overlapping rules, unknown input becoming approval, stuck states, and exclusive splits that meet parallel joins all pass. The Chapter 1 outputs are the criteria that Chapters 3, 6 and 7 later select against, so these weaknesses carry into selection. A constraint's hardness is inferred from its priority, quality needs have to borrow functional requirements, and outcomes cannot be computed. For brownfield work there is no observed-behaviour baseline to diagnose against. Sol stays advisory: it never fills owners, sources, evidence or measure fields, which matches the handbooks' evidence discipline.

**What AIW already does well**

- **Requirements made testable by deterministic rules** — The acceptance check needs at least 20 characters, no bracketed prompts and no statement-check hits, and timing claims need an agreed boundary and threshold (public/requirements-domain.js:98-99). The ambiguity check names subjective words, vague words, regulations cited without an instrument, qualities with no target, and fragments (public/statement-checks.js:7-14, :80-90).
- **Every requirement traces to why and where** — An error is raised unless a requirement links a business outcome and a journey step, or has a person's reviewed journey treatment (public/requirements-domain.js:109-110). Requirements also need an owner and a source (:97). Business drivers in Chapter 2 link outcomes (public/quality-domain.js:166).
- **Unknown is an explicit state, not a value** — A field is stated, not stated or an open question. The state is kept beside the field, never in its text (public/open-fields.js:1-12). Chapter 8 execution agreements keep a missing response 'unresolved' and separate acceptance from the authoritative business outcome (public/composition-manifest.js:63-66).
- **Requirements, assumptions, decisions and test objectives kept apart** — REQ and ASM are separate types (public/requirements-domain.js:18-29). A test objective is told apart from the requirement it verifies (public/statement-checks.js:97; public/requirements-domain.js:104-105). Chapter 3 refuses approval evidence while a linked Chapter 1 assumption is unconfirmed (public/decisions-domain.js:159-165).
- **Typed model and process validation** — RELATION-INVALID checks every recorded relationship against ArchiMate 3.1 typing (public/architecture-model.js:439-453). PROC-* catches dangling flows, uncontracted message flows, missing start or end, unreachable elements, unconditioned exclusive branches and unjoined parallel splits (public/process-model.js:106-152).
- **Provenance-first drafting that never guesses accountability or figures** — The brief interpreter cites passage ids and marks each proposal as stated or inferred, and it never guesses an owner (public/brief-interpreter.js:11-24). Sol never drafts owner, source, evidence or measure fields (public/field-suggestions.js:100-105).
- **Source contradictions retained, not resolved silently** — Process claims carry a source and an exact locator (public/record-fields.js:219). When claims disagree on the same subject and topic, they become Unresolved conflictsWith edges in the canonical graph (public/process-model.js:49-52; public/architecture-model.js:167).
- **Confirmation is not approval** — A record's confirmation lapses when its content changes. The Chapter 1 export says that confirmation records the user's review and is not governance approval (public/requirements-domain.js:309).

### C1-01 · No customer operation (service promise) contract recorded before mechanisms are chosen  
*Severity high · effort L*

- **Handbooks:** Before choosing compute or a mechanism, record each customer operation as a versioned promise. It names the owner who may change it, the eligible population (rejected demand stays visible), the acceptance event, the success boundary (which durable state must exist), the authoritative system, idempotency scope, authorisation freshness, what a lost response means, the recovery obligation for acknowledged work, clock and update rules, and a small terminal state space with a discharge predicate. Acceptance, dispatch and finality are separate events with separate owners.  
  _Refs:_ Cloud Architecture Handbook › Template one Customer operation contract; CLD 01 Business contracts before cloud services › Write the contract as reviewable data (Cloud_Architecture_Handbook__01); Business Architecture Handbook › BIZ-SE-001 Outcome based Business Service Contracts (Business_Architecture_Handbook__03); Application Architecture Master Handbook › Chapter 01 › Translate the boundary into an implementation contract (Application_Architecture_Master__01); Enterprise Architecture Handbook › EA-DR-001 Outcome and Constraint Contracts › Design parameters (Enterprise_Architecture_Handbook__01)
- **AIW today:** A requirement holds one free-text acceptance field (Given/When/Then). It is checked only for length, bracketed prompts and statement-check words. The promise semantics exist only further down the method, one contract at a time. A Chapter 8 execution agreement records repetition, identity, protection, timeout meaning, completion (acceptance or business outcome), discovery and outcome authority. No record ties several contracts to one customer promise. No field anywhere holds eligibility, success boundary, authorisation freshness, the recovery obligation for acknowledged work, clock or pause rules, update obligations, or a promise version per cohort.  
  _Evidence:_ public/record-fields.js:114 — artefact 'acceptance' is a single 4,000-character text field; public/requirements-domain.js:98-99 — the acceptance check reads wording only; public/composition-manifest.js:58-66 — timeout meaning, completion, discovery and outcomeAuthorityId are recorded per Chapter 8 contract; public/requirements-domain.js:18-29 — none of the nine Chapter 1 types is a promise or service contract
- **Why it matters:** Each Chapter 8 agreement can be consistent on its own and still contradict what the customer was promised. For example, one contract may treat a timeout as failed while the promise keeps the outcome open. Components and technology are then chosen against requirements that never state a success boundary or a recovery obligation, which the handbooks require before any service selection.
- **Recommendation:** Add a Chapter 1 'Operation promise' record (OPC) in the Requirements stage. It links to requirements, actors and outcomes and holds: promise owner (a stakeholder), consumer, trigger, eligibility rule and how rejected demand is treated, acceptance event, success boundary (the durable states that must exist), authoritative system (open until Chapter 5 or 8 names it), idempotency scope, authorisation freshness, lost-response meaning (unresolved by default), recovery obligation for acknowledged work, clock start and pause rules, update obligation while pending, terminal outcomes (from C1-02) and a version with its effective cohort. Validate: a Must requirement on a customer-facing journey with no promise raises a warning. In Chapter 8, a contract may cite promiseId, and a deterministic error is raised when its timeout or completion meaning contradicts the promise; a promise that no contract realises raises a warning at the Chapter 8 handoff. Output: SDD §1 prints a 'Customer promises' table and Appendix D prints the full records. Sol may draft the free text from brief passages as a proposal. Owner, eligibility figures and authority stay person-entered (NO_DRAFT).
- **Depends on:** C1-02, deep-dive data-and-integration: Partial: partners have descriptive fields but no capability profile, acknowledgement finality or onboarding

### C1-02 · Journeys have no record of their own: no promised outcome, terminal-state set, closure predicate or explicit unknown outcome  
*Severity high · effort L*

- **Handbooks:** A process is defined by its demand unit and acceptance boundary. Terminal states are distinct (confirmed, declined, cancelled, transferred, known failure), and an unknown outcome stays open. Each terminal state has a closure predicate bound to evidence from the right authority, plus reason codes, and obligations still open inside a case are tracked. A journey states the user goal, the promise it covers, its stopping point and the allowed asynchronous states, records event time separately from observation time, and reconciles a cohort by identity rather than by count.  
  _Refs:_ Business Architecture Handbook › Outcome and Obligation Process Modelling › Forces and mechanism; Technical depth (Business_Architecture_Handbook__02); Quality Architecture Volume 9 › QAE-UB-001 Task completion and outcome oriented journeys (Quality_Architecture_Volume_9_Handbook__02); QA Environment Handbook › QAE 22 End to end journeys; QAE T06 Journey specification (QA_Environment_Handbook__01); Business Architecture Handbook › BIZ-FND-06 Process work states events and obligations (Business_Architecture_Handbook__01); Enterprise Architecture Handbook › EA-REC-01 Measures and acceptance (Enterprise_Architecture_Handbook__04)
- **AIW today:** A journey is only a free-text journeyId (120 characters, marked as a measure field) shared by steps that carry order and processKind. No journey record exists to hold a goal, a promise, a stopping point or an outcome set. BPMN end events carry an event type but no outcome class, and PROC-END checks only that an end exists. Chapter 8 data lifecycles list states and transitions without marking terminal or open states, and their checks catch only broken links. The held and uncertain end events of the bank reference project are hard-coded routes, not a recorded outcome model.  
  _Evidence:_ public/requirements-domain.js:179 — journey TYPE_TEXT holds only journeyId; public/record-fields.js:114 — journeyId is a 120-character text field marked as a measure; public/process-model.js:17-18 — event types and positions only; no outcome class; public/process-model.js:130-131 — PROC-START and PROC-END check only that a start and an end exist; public/process-model.js:28-33, :53-56 — held and uncertain outcomes exist only in the bank-payment reference routes; public/interfaces-domain.js:146-150, :344 — lifecycle states are unclassified; the checks find only missing fields or contracts
- **Why it matters:** Every BPMN path can reach an end event while obligations stay undischarged and unknown outcomes are quietly relabelled as failures. The SDD cannot say when a case is done or who proves it, and the downstream mechanisms (reconciliation, status reads) have no stated target.
- **Recommendation:** Promote the journey to a Chapter 1 record (JNY). It keeps the existing journeyId as its key, and steps are migrated to reference it. The record holds user goal, demand unit, acceptance boundary, the promise it covers (C1-01), stopping point, and an outcome set. Each terminal outcome has a class (fulfilled, declined before acceptance, cancelled by authorised variation, transferred, known failure, unknown or pending), a closure predicate, an authority (open until Chapter 5 or 8), evidence and reason codes. BPMN end events and Chapter 8 lifecycle states name the terminal outcome they represent. Validate: a journey with no outcome set raises a warning. A journey whose Chapter 8 contracts keep a timeout unresolved but which has no unknown outcome raises an error. An end event with no outcome raises a warning. A lifecycle state with no exit that maps to no terminal outcome raises a 'stuck state' warning. An unknown outcome with no owner or next action raises a warning. Model: the journey map shows each outcome set and which end events reach it. Output: SDD §1 journeys print goal, promise and outcomes. Sol may propose outcome classes from contract semantics, as proposals only.
- **Verification:** ch01:C1-02 confirmed

### C1-03 · Exception and recovery paths are not designed: no exception-class record, and Chapter 4 traces failure scenarios only for the reference project  
*Severity high · effort L*

- **Handbooks:** Classify material exceptions as known failure, uncertain outcome or dispute, and design them alongside the normal path. For each, keep the original case identity, record what is known and unknown, and name an owner with decision authority, the next evidence-producing action, a review interval, an escalation horizon, the customer communication and a closure predicate that covers residual obligations. Missing or stale input goes to clarification with an owner and is never treated as false. Exception capacity is sized by effort. Journeys include ordinary interruptions (refresh, duplicate click, network loss, session expiry, device switch).  
  _Refs:_ Business Architecture Handbook › BIZ-VS-004 Exception and Recovery Value Paths; BIZ-FND-04 › Include incomplete and adverse paths (Business_Architecture_Handbook__01); Business Architecture Handbook › Straight through Flow with Explicit Exceptions › Forces and mechanism; Variants and failure response (Business_Architecture_Handbook__02); QA Environment Handbook › QAE 22 End to end journeys (QA_Environment_Handbook__01)
- **AIW today:** The BPMN model can draw error and timer boundary events and gateways. For every project, the Process perspective walks each recorded path from a start to an end (up to 12, processScenarios), so adverse branches can be drawn and traced. Only Chapter 4's recorded responsibility trace (logical.scenario) is success-only for non-reference projects; hold and timeout are fixed step indexes for the bank reference, and the 'Make an uncertain outcome explicit' proposal is bank-only. Chapter 8 contracts hold failure, timeout and retry policy text and a timeout meaning per contract. No record holds an exception class, an owner with decision authority, a response promise, a review interval, an escalation horizon, customer communication, a closure predicate or a return-to-routine guard.  
  _Evidence:_ public/model-scope.js:19-20 — a blank project gets ['success'] only; hold and timeout are fixed indexes; public/logical-domain.js:207 — logical.scenario accepts only success, hold or timeout; public/requirements-domain.js:160-162 — the pending-outcome proposal is gated to the bank-payment template; public/process-model.js:17 — error and timer event types exist, with no exception semantics
- **Why it matters:** For a new project the architect can only demonstrate the happy path. Failed, contested and uncertain cases, where most operational cost and harm sit, have no owner, no deadline and no closure, and Chapter 4 never checks that responsibilities cover them.
- **Recommendation:** Add an exception-class record on a journey (Chapter 1, Journey stage). It holds class (known failure, uncertain outcome, dispute, interruption), trigger step, the case identity kept, known and unknown facts, an owner with decision authority, the next evidence-producing action, a response promise in staffed hours, a review interval, an escalation horizon, customer communication, a closure predicate and a return-to-routine guard. Replace the hard-coded hold and timeout indexes: Chapter 4 derives one trace scenario per exception class, so every project can trace its adverse paths. Validate: a gateway or boundary event leading to a non-fulfilled terminal outcome with no exception class raises a warning, and an exception class without owner or escalation horizon raises a warning. Model: the process picture marks exception paths. Output: SDD §1 lists exception classes per journey. Sol may propose candidate classes from Chapter 8 contracts whose timeout is unresolved, as proposals only.
- **Depends on:** C1-02
- **Verification:** ch01:C1-03 overstated (The cited code is correct. Blank projects get ['success'] only, and hold and timeout are fixed indexes (public/model-scope.js:19-20). logical.scenario accepts only success, hold or timeout (public/logical-domain.js:207). The pending-outcome proposal is gated to the bank template (requirements-domain.js:160-162). But 'for a new project the architect can only demonstrate the happy path' is overstate)

### C1-04 · Requirement record lacks consequence if violated, protected invariant and its authority, challenge method, rationale, status and revisit trigger  
*Severity medium · effort M*

- **Handbooks:** Keep one record per consequential requirement. It holds the business outcome, the consequence if violated (which irreversible effect can follow), the scope, the hard invariant with the exact protected population, the authority, decision, local evidence, target obligations, owner and revisit trigger. Each requirement and assumption also states how it is challenged (replay concurrently, inject a crash around commit, restore and reconcile identity sets), so a diagram cannot turn an untested assumption into an apparent guarantee. Finally, the record says which durable records constitute acceptance and what a failure means (provider timeout → UNKNOWN).

- **AIW today:** A requirement has title, description, owner, source, priority with its basis, one acceptance text and imported attributes. An imported requirement also keeps the source's assertions (delivery, validation, evidence, UAT counts), which source-assurance checks read. A test-objective record can be linked to a requirement with 'verifies'. A record can be confirmed, or retired with a reason and restored. There is no consequence-if-violated, protected-invariant link, change authority, verification-method field, rationale, agreed, deferred or rejected status, version or revisit trigger. Chapter 8 invariants bind data and contracts but cannot name the requirement they protect.  
  _Evidence:_ public/record-fields.js:114 — artefact fields: no consequence, invariant, authority, method, rationale or trigger; public/requirements-domain.js:97-99, :109-110 — the requirement checks cover accountability, acceptance wording and links only; public/record-fields.js:164 — interfaces.invariant links dataIds and contractIds, not requirements; public/interfaces-domain.js:189 — data definitions carry requirementIds (one-way)

These fields feed the dive's verification hop; they do not replace it. Output: Appendix D prints them, and SDD §1 lists requirements with irreversible consequences. Sol may draft consequence and challenge text as proposals; authority and status are person-only.

### C1-05 · Constraint register is shallow: no predicate, scope, change authority, harm prevented or class, and hardness is inferred from priority  
*Severity medium · effort M*

- **Handbooks:** Keep a constraint register: predicate, scope (the predicate's domain), the owner or authority who may change it, the harm it prevents, rationale and evidence. Classify each statement as a hard constraint, a negotiable objective (with unit and preference direction), an assumption or a preference. Keep a threshold apart from its preference curve (a cost cap is not 'cheaper is better'), and treat a changed constraint as a new, versioned decision problem. Type constraints (deadlines, commitments, skills, data location, budget, transition tolerance) before choosing a pattern.  
  _Refs:_ Quality Architecture Vol 9 › QAE-OS-003 Hard constraints and negotiable objectives › How the mechanism works; Technical depth (Quality_Architecture_Volume_9_Handbook__01); Application Architecture Handbook › How to use this volume › From desired outcome to demonstrated improvement (Application_Architecture_Handbook__01)
- **AIW today:** A CON record has only the common fields (title, description, owner, source, priority, acceptance). A constraint driver is hard unless every constraint it links is Should or Could, so an unstated priority binds. There is no predicate, scope, change authority, harm prevented, kind, or classification against objective or preference. Chapter 2 quality drivers do hold a comparator, value and unit (a threshold with a direction), but a quality driver cannot be made hard. A changed constraint does reopen linked drivers and decisions through source-change fingerprints, which partly meets 'new decision problem'.  
  _Evidence:_ public/requirements-domain.js:18-29 — no constraint subtype or kind; public/record-fields.js:114 — CON has only the common artefact fields; public/decision-reading.js:10-12, :145-150 — constraintForce derives hard or soft from priority; an unstated priority binds; public/record-fields.js:126 — quality drivers hold operator, targetValue and unit; public/quality-domain.js:104-110 — source-change fingerprints on Chapter 1 records
- **Why it matters:** The decision reading and the Chapter 7 assessments rule alternatives out on a constraint whose hardness is inferred from a priority label. A reviewer cannot see what predicate is protected, who may relax it or what harm it prevents, so the hard gates in selection are neither defensible nor challengeable.
- **Recommendation:** Give CON explicit fields: class (hard constraint, negotiable objective or preference), kind (regulatory, contractual, technical, budget, schedule, organisational, data location, skills), predicate, scope, changeAuthority (a stakeholder), harmPrevented, rationale and evidence. Migrate constraintForce to read the explicit class. Existing records are seeded once from priority with the basis 'migrated from priority', so no second authority path runs beside it, and 'hard by inference' shows as an item to confirm until a person states the class. Validate: a hard constraint without a predicate or change authority raises a warning. A negotiable objective must link or create a Chapter 2 driver with a comparator and unit. A preference with no threshold never acts as a gate. A predicate change bumps the version, and the existing source-change reopens Chapters 2 and 3. Model: the Boundary view shows class and authority. Output: SDD §2 prints the register; today constraints appear only through drivers. Knowledge never sets the class; released regulation records remain offers.
- **Depends on:** deep-dive enterprise-alignment: No organisation units, decision rights or delegation envelopes; owners and approvers are free text
- **Verification:** ch01:C1-05 confirmed

### C1-06 · Business rules hold one value: no decision table with input domains, hit policy, no-hit or default semantics, unknown-input handling, or overlap and gap checks  
*Severity medium · effort L*

- **Handbooks:** Express policy decisions as decision tables (DMN 1.3). Declare input types, units, allowed values and evidence status, and validate inputs before evaluation. Choose a hit policy deliberately (Unique, First, Collect), state inclusive or exclusive boundaries, and keep no-hit, explicit default and input-validation failure distinct, each with an accountable response. Test for gaps, overlaps (with a concrete witness) and exact endpoints. Unknown, stale or out-of-range input must never become an approval. Changing the hit policy or row order is a semantic change that needs business approval. Routing and decision logic share one eligibility definition, and criteria declare strict or inclusive thresholds and a truth table for missing facts.  
  _Refs:_ Business Architecture Handbook › Decision Tables with Explicit Hit and Default Semantics › Forces and mechanism; Technical depth (Business_Architecture_Handbook__02); Business Architecture Handbook › Composition recipes › BIZ-REC-05, BIZ-REC-06; Comparative design essays › BIZ-ESS-03 (Business_Architecture_Handbook__03); Business Architecture Handbook › Technical laboratories › BIZ-LAB-05; Reusable decision record › 12 Policy and decisions (Business_Architecture_Handbook__04); Business Architecture Handbook › BIZ-FND-07 Policy decisions and control evidence (Business_Architecture_Handbook__01); Application Architecture Handbook › Specification (APP-MD-010); Strategy (APP-CD-003) › Scoped technical parameters (Application_Architecture_Handbook__02)
- **AIW today:** A business rule (BR) holds value, unit, currency, effectiveDate, authority, instrument and section, plus refinesId, dataId, controlId and requirementIds. BPMN exclusive and inclusive gateways need a free-text condition on each outgoing flow, and nothing links a gateway to a rule. No decision table, hit policy, input domain or unknown-input semantics exists anywhere in the source. Model import reads BPMN gateways but not DMN.  
  _Evidence:_ public/requirements-domain.js:177-178, :210-213 — rule fields and single links; public/process-model.js:146 — PROC-GATEWAY only requires condition text on each branch; public/model-interpreter.js:488 — BPMN gateways imported; no DMN reader; grep -rn 'dmn|hit policy|decision table' public/*.js finds no rule structure (only a font blob)
- **Why it matters:** Eligibility and approval logic is where unknown inputs quietly become approvals. AIW cannot detect overlapping or missing rule rows, and gateway conditions in the process can drift from the business rule with no finding. An architect cannot show that the decision logic the components will implement is complete and unambiguous.
- **Recommendation:** Let a BR be kind 'parameter' (today's shape) or 'decision table'. A decision table holds inputs [{name, type, unit, allowed range or values, missing or stale handling: clarify, reject or explicit row}], hitPolicy (Unique, First, Priority, Collect), rows with inclusive or exclusive bounds and an outcome, a no-hit outcome with an owner, a separate default row, and a version. Add deterministic, explainable Validate checks in the style of statement-checks.js. Overlaps under Unique are reported with a witness. Gaps over declared numeric or enumerated domains are reported. Endpoints that no row covers are reported. 'Missing input reaches an approving outcome' is an error. A hit-policy or row-order change creates a new version and raises source-change on linked requirements, gateways and controls. An exclusive gateway may name its decision table (decisionTableId), with a warning when its branch conditions do not match the table's outcomes. Model: a table view of the rule. Output: Appendix D prints the tables. An optional DMN 1.3 import arrives as proposals a person keeps. Sol may propose rows from source passages as proposals and never fills thresholds.

### C1-07 · Outcome definitions lack outcome classes (late, failed, unknown), an observation cut, versioning with a bridge, and separation of output acceptance from outcome attainment  
*Severity medium · effort M*

- **Handbooks:** An outcome is a versioned tuple: purpose, population rule, intent identity, acceptance event, desired outcome, deadline, evidence predicate, constraints, owner and review rule. Measures run over a fixed eligible population, and attempts and intents use separate denominators. Timely, late, failed and unknown classes are reported separately, and unknown is never relabelled as failure. Event time and observation time stay distinct, and a definition change publishes a bridge and a restatement. Delivery acceptance of an output stays separate from outcome attainment, with its own owner. Guardrails and the time window are fixed before a platform is chosen.  
  _Refs:_ Business Architecture Handbook › BIZ-FND-11 › Write the measurement contract; BIZ-IV-001 › Forces and mechanism (Business_Architecture_Handbook__01); Enterprise Architecture Handbook › EA-DR-001 Outcome and Constraint Contracts › Measures and validation (Enterprise_Architecture_Handbook__01); Enterprise Architecture Handbook › EA-REC-01 Measures and acceptance; EA-XD-01 Evidence and decision; EA-LAB-12 (Enterprise_Architecture_Handbook__04); Integration Architecture Vol 7 › Decision record › INT-DEC-01 Objective (Integration_Architecture_Volume_7_Handbook__05); Quality Architecture Volume 9 › QAE-UB-001 Task completion and outcome oriented journeys (Quality_Architecture_Volume_9_Handbook__02)
- **AIW today:** An outcome holds problem, measure, baseline, target and 'by' as free text; the measure fields are ones Sol may not draft. SDD §1 prints them.   _Refs:_ Business Architecture Handbook › Straight through Flow with Explicit Exceptions › Forces and mechanism; Implementation sketch (Business_Architecture_Handbook__02); Business Architecture Handbook › BIZ-FND-07 › Make table semantics explicit (Business_Architecture_Handbook__01)
- **AIW today:** The BPMN reader records a gateway's default flow and marks that sequence flow isDefault. When the journey drafter adopts imported flows as process transitions, it drops isDefault. - **Why it matters:** A missing routing condition is exactly where unknown or incomplete input slips onto a routine or approving path. Turning the gap into text that the check accepts hides it from the architect and from Validate, and loses the default-path semantics the source model stated.

### C1-14 · No benefit claim ledger: counterfactual, benefit type, release condition, realisation owner, disbenefits and de-duplication are not recorded  
*Severity low · effort M*

- **Handbooks:** Every material saving traces to its source, measurement type and unit. Each benefit claim records a counterfactual, a causal mechanism, a release condition and date, a realisation owner, and its overlap with other initiatives. Cash avoidance, released capacity, allocation changes and qualitative benefits stay separate, and a shared release event counts once. Weak evidence is published as ranges or conditional claims. Each benefit has one identity, an owner, a baseline, dependencies, disbenefits and a horizon, with contribution explanations instead of duplicated attribution.  
  _Refs:_ Enterprise Architecture Handbook › Architecture economics and option analysis › EA-EC-005 Precise Scores and Savings from Unsupported Assumptions (Enterprise_Architecture_Handbook__03); Business Architecture Handbook › Several projects claim the same benefit (BIZ-XD-09); Reusable decision record › 23 Benefits and ownership (Business_Architecture_Handbook__04)
- **AIW today:** Only the outcome's free-text fields and the persona's 'Expected benefits' text exist, and value-outcome delivery observations map to requirements. To its credit, AIW computes no hidden benefit score.  
  _Evidence:_ public/requirements-domain.js:172, :176 — persona benefits and outcome fields are free text; public/delivery-feedback.js:13-14 — value-outcome observations map to requirement ids; grep for counterfactual, disbenefit or benefit owner finds no business-benefit record; public/composition-knowledge.js:25 uses 'counterfactual' only for design options
- **Why it matters:** Business cases attached to architecture decisions overstate savings when benefits are counted twice or claimed without a release condition. The SDD cannot show what must happen for a saving to become real.
- **Recommendation:** Add a benefit record linked to an outcome. It holds type (cash avoidance, released capacity, allocation change, qualitative), unit, counterfactual, causal mechanism (links to requirements and decisions), release condition and date, realisation owner, dependencies, disbenefits, a contribution statement and evidence strength (point, range or conditional). Validate: a benefit without a counterfactual or realisation owner raises a warning. Two benefits citing the same release event raise an item to confirm. A point value with weak evidence raises a warning to state a range. AIW never totals across types. Output: SDD §1 prints benefits with their conditions. Sol drafts no figures.

### C1-19 · Stakeholder value claims do not separate buyer, user, beneficiary, payer and burden bearer  
*Severity low · effort S*

- **Handbooks:** A value claim relates a stakeholder, a situation, a desired result and an offered change, and names the beneficiary and the burden bearer. Buyer, user, beneficiary and payer stay separate rather than being blended into one score, and segments are defined by needs and context.  
  _Refs:_ Business Architecture Handbook › BIZ-IV-002 Stakeholder Value Proposition Design (Business_Architecture_Handbook__01)
- **AIW today:** Stakeholders and actors carry a category and persona texts (role, motivations, wishes, concerns, usage, benefits). There are no beneficiary, payer or burden-bearer roles relating them to outcomes, and persona statements have no source of their own.  
  _Evidence:_ public/requirements-domain.js:172-173 — persona fields and user categories; public/requirements-domain.js:32 — no value-role relationship kinds

Persona statements use the existing open or not-stated state plus a source. Validate: where the brief mentions cost or effort, an outcome with a beneficiary but no named burden bearer raises an item to confirm. Output: the SDD persona table adds role columns.
