# Chapter 2 · Architectural drivers

Part of the AIW handbook gap review (AIW-HANDBOOK-GAP-REVIEW-2026-10-30.md). Gaps verified against the AIW source (paths relative to AIW-V5-Local-Source) and challenged by a skeptic; handbook references are "Handbook › heading (section id)".


**How AIW compares with the handbooks.** Chapter 2 covers the first half of the quality-attribute method the handbooks prescribe, and covers it well. It has one register of drivers by kind with stable codes. A structured scenario and target must pass a measurability check before a person can confirm it. Source fingerprints reopen drivers, and driver changes reopen conflicts. The utility tree is built from the SA Playbook and shows uncovered attributes as holes, and a What-if carries a target change through to plans, capabilities, products and decisions. All of this is deterministic, and Sol is barred from measure fields. The handbooks then expect each priority scenario to become a falsifiable contract and a design obligation: a set of normal, peak, failure, recovery and change scenarios; a measurement contract with population, statistic, clock anchors, guardrails and versioned definitions; a recorded workload model; journey promises decomposed into child budgets that compose; and, for each priority driver, a recorded causal mechanism and evidence plan. AIW keeps most of this as free text inside one scenario and one target. Where it reasons further (desk capacity, budgets, design questions, monitoring drafts), it infers its inputs from target wording rather than reading recorded facts, and it checks each part against the whole availability budget instead of composing the journey. Its driver-to-carrier test counts only Ch6/7/10 records that carry a figure. As a result, correctness, security and traceability drivers read as uncarried even when Chapter 5 and 8 mechanisms deliver them. About 35 of the 98 reader items restate the measurement-contract and SLO gaps the deep-dives already hold (quality-method, operability-and-delivery). This shard adds handbook support to those gaps through dependsOn and isolates what the dives missed.

### C2-01 · No mechanism claim or evidence plan per driver, and the ARS 'carried' test cannot see how correctness, security and traceability drivers are met  
*Severity medium · effort M*

- **Handbooks:** Every priority quality scenario needs a specific causal explanation: which mechanism delivers it and why. It also needs an owner and an evidence plan, for example one accepted identity → scoped unique key and transaction → concurrent replay outcomes. Naming a pattern or a component is not enough. Validation walks each priority scenario and confirms that every critical response has an owner and a mechanism. Templates record the selected catalogue mechanisms, the rejected claim with its causal reason, the verification and revisit_when.  
  _Refs:_ Application Architecture Handbook › Attribute Driven Design › Measures and validation; Quality Attribute Scenarios › Measures and validation (Application_Architecture_Handbook__07); Software Application Design Handbook › SAD 03 › Map quality conditions to mechanisms / Validate mechanisms independently (Software_Application_Design_Handbook__01); Software Design Component Dictionary › Worked design templates › Template 9 Non-functional requirement and trade-off record (Software_Design_Component_Dictionary_Master__02); Application Architecture Master Handbook › Worked design templates › Template 2 Quality attribute scenario and tradeoff (Application_Architecture_Master__02); Business Architecture Handbook › BIZ-IV-001 Motivation and Outcome Traceability › Structure / Technical depth; BIZ-FND-02 › Make the mechanism contestable (Business_Architecture_Handbook__01); Cloud Infrastructure Master › Worked design templates › CIF-TPL04 (Cloud_Infrastructure_Master__02); Cloud Infrastructure Master Handbook › CIF Method 03 › Build a quality scenario register (Cloud_Infrastructure_Master__01); Data Architecture Handbook › DAT-AP-006/007/008 › Measures and validation (Data_Architecture_Handbook__04)
- **AIW today:** A driver holds one free-text candidate tactic. Mechanism claims exist but sit in several chapters, not on the driver. (1) Ch3 alternatives record effect, reason and evidence per driver, and Validate errors when the chosen alternative leaves any named driver without a reason or evidence (decisions-domain.js:126). (2) Ch5 components hold quality properties (concern or driver, statement, mechanism, source) and serves{driverId, reason}. (3) Design-task solutions carry driverIds, a mechanism, obligations and why/verify per effect. (4) The design-question rule warns per component, capability or realization when a question a driver opens is unanswered. Still missing: a per-driver mechanism and evidence-plan record or check for every High-priority driver; drivers named by no decision get no reasoning check. The ARS and Ch11 'carried' test counts only a Ch6 capability driverId, a Ch7 target or a Ch10 plan target, and those kinds are availability, recovery time, data loss, latency, throughput and capacity. An integrity, security or traceability driver can be named there only against an ill-fitting kind, which reads 'not compared'. 'Verified' means only that the measurement text is not empty.  
  _Evidence:_ public/record-fields.js:126 — tactic is free text with a picker; public/record-fields.js:144; public/realisation-domain.js:33-35, :198-213 — component quality properties (mechanism, source) and serves with reason; public/architecture-obligations.js:57-73 — design-task solutions with driverIds, mechanism, obligations, verification state; public/design-questions.js:157-160 — design-question warning per component, capability or realization; public/ars-document.js:77-80, :95, :172 — carried = named by a Ch6/7/10 driverId field only; public/driver-links.js:22, :161-168 — target kinds are availability, recovery-time, data-loss, latency, throughput, capacity; the carriers are Ch6 recovery, Ch7 targets and Ch10 plan targets; public/ars-document.js:35 — 'verified' = measurement text non-empty; public/sdd-architecture.js:1158-1166 — per-driver component properties printed, with no causal claim or evidence plan; public/utility-model.js:164-165 — 'carried by no component' is a Model observation, not a check
- **Why it matters:** An integrity, security or traceability driver (for example bank QD-001 'At most 0 extra debits') can never count as carried, because no target kind fits it. The gate therefore reports it uncarried even when Ch5 and Ch8 mechanisms deliver it. Meanwhile a driver named on a plan passes without any explanation of how the plan meets it. Reviewers cannot see why the chosen components, technology and deployment satisfy each priority scenario, which is the core justification an SDD has to give.
- **Recommendation:** Add `mechanisms[]` to quality.driver. Each row holds: carrierRef (a Ch3 chosen alternative, Ch5 component property, Ch8 contract policy or invariant, Ch9 control or Ch10 plan); a causal claim (whose behaviour changes and why); the strongest competing explanation or failure condition; evidenceRef (the verification record from the deep-dive, or a Ch11 typed check); and owner. AIW proposes candidate rows deterministically from existing serves, properties and design-task solutions, and a person keeps or dismisses each one. In the ARS, 'carried' becomes 'has a recorded mechanism' in any chapter, and the Ch6/7/10 rows are reported separately as 'carried with a figure'. Validate: a Ch2 warning and Ch11 item to confirm when a High-priority quality driver has no mechanism with a causal claim and an evidence plan, and 'name the mechanism' when a row holds only a pattern name. Sol's 'challenge' preset may draft the competing explanation as a proposal; it never records the claim. SDD §2 prints 'Delivered by' and 'Evidence plan' per driver.
- **Depends on:** deep-dive quality-method: Tactics are one free-text string, inferred by keyword, with thin coverage and no applicability or contraindications, deep-dive traceability: No verification or test hop: acceptance and measurement are free text, and evidence never reaches the trail, deep-dive traceability: The trail is a set of records per layer, not a recorded causal chain, so it both omits and over-includes records
- **Verification:** ch02:C2-01 overstated (The core holds. Gate code: ars-document.js:77-78 (carriedDrivers = linksIndex keys), :95 and the arsGate item 'carried by no runtime plan' at ~:173. ### C2-02 · One scenario per driver and no environment class: no normal/peak/failure/recovery/change scenario set and no check for adverse cases  
*Severity medium · effort M*

- **Handbooks:** For each quality concern, write a set of scenarios: at least ordinary completion, failure after a possible effect, peak load, recovery and a likely change, plus exploratory scenarios that use ranges and research questions. Each scenario's environment states its demand, failure mode and transition state, has a measurable response and an owner, and defines the accepted degraded mode before the exercise. Change scenarios form a fixed corpus that is replayed against each boundary alternative.  
  _Refs:_ Vol 10 › FND-02 Translate business promises into measurable requirements (Applied_Solution_Architecture_Volume_10_Handbook__01); Quality Architecture Vol 9 › Foundations › QAE-FND-02; QAE-OS-002 Quality attribute scenarios and measurable promises (Quality_Architecture_Volume_9_Handbook__01); Quality Architecture Volume 9 › Reusable working templates › QAE-TPL-01 Quality scenario card; QAE-LAB-01 (Quality_Architecture_Volume_9_Handbook__03); Quality Architecture Volume 9 › QAE-CO-001 Change scenarios coupling and modular boundaries; QAE-CO-006 (Quality_Architecture_Volume_9_Handbook__02); Application Architecture Handbook › Quality Attribute Scenarios › Forces and mechanism; Implementation; Variants and failure response (Application_Architecture_Handbook__07); Enterprise Architecture Handbook › EA-DR-002 › Implementation / Design parameters (scenario environment) (Enterprise_Architecture_Handbook__01); Cloud Infrastructure Master Handbook › CIF Method 03 › Build a quality scenario register (Cloud_Infrastructure_Master__01); Cloud Infrastructure Master › Worked design templates › CIF-TPL04 (Cloud_Infrastructure_Master__02); Technology Architecture Handbook › TEC-FND-01 Business outcomes and the operating contract (Technology_Architecture_Handbook__01)
- **AIW today:** A quality driver carries exactly one scenario: its scenarioId QS-nnn mirrors the driver id. Stimulus, conditions and response are free text. There is no environment class or scenario type. The scenario check only tests that the three texts exist. The only prompt toward adverse cases is the 'Test a boundary condition' proposal, which appends one sentence to conditions. The utility tree has one leaf per driver. A change scenario cannot be reused across Ch3 alternatives or Ch4/Ch5 boundaries. The workaround is extra drivers, which split priority and conflicts.  
  _Evidence:_ public/quality-domain.js:258 — scenarioId 'QS-'+id: one scenario per driver; public/record-fields.js:126 — stimulus, conditions, response free text; no environment or scenario-type field; public/quality-domain.js:176 — the 'scenario' check tests only that the three texts are present; public/quality-domain.js:219 — the 'boundary' proposal appends text to conditions; public/utility-model.js:31-54 — one tree leaf per driver
- **Why it matters:** Mechanisms for retries, failover, degradation and recovery are derived from adverse scenarios. When a High-priority concern is written only for normal load, nothing in AIW notices, and the component, technology and deployment choices are justified against the easy case.
- **Recommendation:** Add `environment` (normal | peak | degraded dependency | failure after effect | recovery | change | abuse or security | exploratory) and `acceptedDegradedMode` to the scenario. Let a quality driver hold 1 to 12 scenario rows under one priority, each with stimulus, conditions, response and its own target row (reusing the Ch7/Ch10 target-row shape). Exploratory rows may hold a range and are left out of 'measurable' and of decision weights. Validate warnings: a High-priority availability, recoverability, integrity, performance or scalability driver with no adverse scenario; a maintainability or deployability driver with no change scenario. Model: utility-tree leaves per scenario, grouped by environment. Ch3: change-scenario rows can be assessed per alternative (effect, affected owners and artefacts), as driver effects are today. Sol may propose variant scenarios (text only, no figures) as proposals a person keeps. SDD §2 prints the scenarios by environment.
- **Depends on:** deep-dive quality-method: The scenario has no source or artefact field and no measurement contract (population, statistic, cutoff, unknowns), deep-dive worked-example: One target per driver; a business-owned threshold cannot be an open question
- **Verification:** ch02:C2-02 confirmed

### C2-03 · No recorded workload model: demand classes, intent versus attempt, burst profile and skew are inferred from target text  
*Severity medium · effort L*

- **Handbooks:** Characterise the workload before making any capacity or latency claim. That means named operations and their queries; demand classes by the work they need (interactive, accepted asynchronous, control, recovery); workload as class volume × handling time; intent versus retry multipliers; representative peaks, burst size and duration, seasonality, tenant skew and background work. Then test objectives against constrained resources with explicit arithmetic: a 180/s burst against a 90/s dispatch budget makes a 120 s queue-age objective infeasible. A percentile reported without its offered load hides overload.  
  _Refs:_ Data Architecture Handbook › Technical foundations › DAT-FND-05 Access paths, query cost and amplification (Data_Architecture_Handbook__01); Technology Architecture Handbook › TEC-FND-01 Business outcomes and the operating contract; The decision workflow (Technology_Architecture_Handbook__01); Business Architecture Handbook › Demand capacity and flow › BIZ-DC-001 Demand Segmentation and Shaping (Business_Architecture_Handbook__03); Development Environment Handbook › DEV 29 › Work a latency claim; DEV 27 › Work a parallelism calculation (Development_Environment_Handbook__01); Software Design Component Dictionary Master › SCD-CH03 › Calculate a capacity contradiction; SCD-CH08 (Software_Design_Component_Dictionary_Master__01); QA Environment Handbook › QAE 25 Performance modelling › Define acceptance conditions; Trace a misleading percentile (QA_Environment_Handbook__01); Enterprise Component Library Pt1 › BP 01-04 › Assumptions and drivers (Enterprise_Component_Library_Part_1_Application_and_Resilience__02); Business Architecture Handbook › BIZ-LAB-09 Pooling capacity and protecting service classes (Business_Architecture_Handbook__04)
- **AIW today:** The desk and the scale readings infer load from a driver's unit and text by regex (scaleTarget). Failing that they use one Chapter 11 objective (concurrent users or requests a second, with a surge flag) and assume a default 3× peak. Ch7 workloadProfile is free text, and design-task analyses take an arrival rate per task. Some feasibility arithmetic exists: connection totals against product maxConnections, single-unit ceilings, consumer-outage backlog and backlog drain time. But nothing records demand classes, mix, burst duration, skew, retry multipliers or background work, and no queue-age or completion objective is tested against burst arithmetic.  
  _Evidence:_ public/desk-capacity.js:396-411 — scaleTarget reads a rate, users, volume or latency from unit and text by regex; public/desk-capacity.js:77-91 — objective = desk exploration, else Ch11 review objective, else first Chapter 2 driver; public/desk-capacity.js:374, :450-452 — default peak factor 3 as an assumption; public/review-domain.js:419 — review.objective: kind users or rate, value, assumptions, perReplica, surge; public/design-spec.js:97 — Ch7 configuration workloadProfile is free text; public/quality-analysis.js:5-11, :41 — per-task arrival rates; backlog drain warning; public/desk-capacity.js:214-221, :265-266, :190-203, :280-293 — connection, ceiling and backlog readings (partial feasibility); public/record-fields.js:126 — no workload fields on a driver
- **Why it matters:** Product, topology and replica choices in Chapters 7 and 10 rest on these inputs. When they are inferred from target wording they can be confidently wrong: the dives found 15-minute deadlines read as request latency. A burst or a shift in demand mix that breaks a promise stays invisible.
- **Recommendation:** Add a Chapter 2 `quality.workload` record holding demand classes {name, unit of work (intent), volume and period, peak factor, burst rate and duration, retry or attempt multiplier, payload size, tenant skew, freshness}, plus background and recovery work, source and confirmation, linked to requirements and quality drivers. The desk and scale readings read it first; when they fall back to today's inference, they label it 'inferred from target text'. Validate: a warning for a performance, scalability or capacity driver with no linked workload class; an item to confirm, with the arithmetic shown, when a confirmed queue-age or completion target is infeasible under the recorded burst against the recorded or assumed processing budget. Workload figures are measure fields Sol never drafts; Sol may propose class names from the requirements. SDD §2 prints the workload table, and the capacity text cites it.
- **Depends on:** deep-dive quality-method: No SLO, SLI or error-budget record; budgets are calculated readings, not owned allocations; percentile targets are split as if they add

### C2-04 · No journey-level composition: each part is checked against the whole availability budget, and drivers cannot be broken into child budgets  
*Severity medium · effort L*

- **Handbooks:** Break a parent driver into child scenarios along the execution path, with time and tail budgets, a visible reserve and allowances for queues and handoffs. Then check that the children recompose (serial sum, parallel max, union bound for tails). Compute journey percentiles from paired per-request observations. Multiply availabilities only under declared independence, after listing shared causes. Treat end-to-end completion as the intersection of obligations over one cohort, never as a product of marginal rates. A component SLO cannot stand in for the journey promise.

- **AIW today:** Availability is read per runtime plan against the whole budget, so N serial parts can each read 'holds'. Sol may propose a decomposition as proposals; the figures stay person-entered. SDD §2 prints the allocation tree.

### C2-06 · No clock anchors or separate clocks: start, stop and pause events, and acceptance versus completion versus finality, cannot be stated  
*Severity medium · effort M*
