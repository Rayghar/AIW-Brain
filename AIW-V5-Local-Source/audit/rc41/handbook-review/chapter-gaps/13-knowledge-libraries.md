# Knowledge catalogue, component libraries and governance

Part of the AIW handbook gap review (AIW-HANDBOOK-GAP-REVIEW-2026-10-30.md). Gaps verified against the AIW source (paths relative to AIW-V5-Local-Source) and challenged by a skeptic; handbook references are "Handbook › heading (section id)".


**How AIW compares with the handbooks.** On provenance and authority, AIW's knowledge governance does what the handbooks ask and in places more: exact hashed passages, four-eyes review re-checked on every read, checksummed and signed releases, withdrawal impact, honest curation marks, and candidate, discovery and catalogue content that never scores. It already has much of the machinery the handbook method needs. That includes a three-valued requires/excludes applicability engine, eight code-authored decision models (five composition topics plus interaction, reads and persistence) whose options carry applicability, 'reconsider if', obligations and verification that flow through a reviewed task into Chapter 11, governed design-concern claims that become Chapter 3 comparisons, and drift findings on reused library guidance. The break is between knowledge and the design records. Outside the built-in models, adopting a pattern, style or library entry in Chapter 3 or 5 is at most an identity check. Chapters 8 to 10 have no picker, and the Playbook and the catalogue hold the same mechanisms under two unconnected identities. Fit against an entry's conditions appears only as a transient contextual reading. Its obligations, parameters, companions and conflicts never reach Validate, the accepted-obligation register or the SDD. The handbooks' common entry template (decision rules, guarantee boundary, variants, parameters, diagnostic signals, validation scenarios) has no AIW shape. Neither do composition recipes or reference-architecture dossiers, their main reuse vehicles, so a design cannot be assembled from reusable knowledge, tailored against it or checked for conformance with it. Most of the 328 catalogue records hold unmarked generator template text, and one person can author and approve a project library entry, which sits beside the four-eyes path for claims. Source discipline is close to the handbooks apart from checked dates and re-validation on claims. Catalogue maintenance (successors, admission checklists, stewards, change registers) and coverage of delivery, operations and transition decisions are only partial.

### K-01 · Adopting a pattern, style or library entry is never checked against the entry's own conditions outside the built-in decision models  
*Severity high · effort L*

- **Handbooks:** Every entry states when to select it: use when, avoid or reconsider when, and testable prerequisites such as release cadence, invariant scope and operational maturity. It also gives decision rules (condition, candidate, contraindication, evidence needed) and a credible alternative with the condition under which that alternative wins. An entry is adopted only after these are checked against the case, and quality lenses never become a score.

### K-02 · Adopting an entry carries none of its obligations, exclusions or validation scenarios into the design outside the built-in decision models  
*Severity high · effort L*

- **Handbooks:** Each entry states a guarantee boundary: a bounded claim, what it depends on and what it does not cover. 'Does not cover' items point to complementary controls that remain obligations; mTLS needs separate authorization, and a reporting projection needs observation time, checkpoint, rebuild, a lag limit and authorization. Entries also carry failure modes with detection and response, Given/When/Then validation scenarios with the evidence required and a falsification experiment. These become the project's verification work.

The built-in topics carry roles (public/composition-knowledge.js:102-108), applicability (:112-125), companion duties such as consumer duplicate protection and retry safety (:126-140, :153-157) and effects with a verification step. A reviewed task applies them and writes Ch3-8 records (public/composition-design.js:1-10; public/architecture-solution.js:13-70). There is no governed (releasable) recipe type; see K-04.
- **AKR entries 'have no' use-when / avoid-when / prerequisites / failure modes / obligations / validation fields (knowledge-b items 3, 4, 7-10, 13, 17-24, 26-29, 31, 33-38)** — The schema has these fields. In public/brain-catalogue.js:353, PAT-CIRCUIT-BREAKER carries context, prerequisites, applicabilityRules, exclusions ('Do not use without timeouts'), risks, failureModes, obligations with verificationHint, alternatives, complements, conflicts, qualityImpacts with conditions, and fitnessTests. public/knowledge-structure.js:18-36 interprets them. Caveat: 318 of 328 records hold template text (K-06), and the fields are displayed, never evaluated (K-01).
- **Decision rules and contraindications travelling with an option: 'condition → candidate → contraindication → evidence needed' (knowledge-a items 37, 64; knowledge-b items 32, 40)** — Recipe predicates carry failure (contraindication) text and block the option (public/composition-knowledge.js:110-125, :150-169). A governed architectureUnit holds typed requires/excludes predicates, exclusions and obligations {action, evidence} (public/architecture-knowledge-unit.js:22-49, :70-79). Released design-concern options carry 'evidence it needs' and per-criterion consequences (public/domain-knowledge.js:17-21, :61-76). Missing: a per-candidate decision-rule table (K-08) and evaluation at adoption (K-01).
- **Chapter 3 assessments seeded from a pattern's stated effects (knowledge-a item 29)** — public/decision-suggestions.js:1-12, :35-40 suggest effects from a linked eligible released claim's architectureUnit effects and from Playbook style/pattern marks. A person records them, and suggestions are never counted. They ignore the claim's applicability; see K-10.
- **Applicability conditions such as payload, region or instance size on claims (knowledge-a item 22)** — Architectural claims must record conditions and limitations (public/knowledge-governance.js:172). Units scope product, version, topology, tier, operating model and capability, and take typed predicates over any configuration fact (public/architecture-knowledge-unit.js:9, :20-49; public/architecture-facts.js:48-61). Project measurements become non-current when the model or analytical inputs change (public/quality-analysis.js:87-98).
- **Recurring decision option tables and decision points with credible alternatives (knowledge-a item 69; knowledge-b items 16, 63)** — Built-in topics cover delivery (direct, queue after commit, outbox), structure (modular vs services), resilience, distribution and partitioning (public/composition-knowledge.js:31-90). Released design-concern claims become Chapter 3 comparison tasks (public/domain-knowledge.js:13-24, :61-76; public/architecture-design.js:29, :41-57). The handbooks' client, rollout, orchestration, delivery and migration-cutover content is still to be imported; see K-17.
- **Pressure-to-mechanism heuristics and adjacent-role distinctions (knowledge-a items 58, 70)** — Partly: Pattern DNA's 12 questions (public/pattern-dna.js:60-216). The distribution recipe treats reverse proxy and load balancing as compatible roles (public/composition-knowledge.js:17, :51-62). The partitioning recipe says read load alone does not justify shards and asks for evidence (:64-67). See K-16.
- **Reverse 'used-in' index from a source or claim to the design, and a per-element source crosswalk (knowledge-a items 55, 80; knowledge-b items 50, 61)** — public/knowledge-governance.js:101-113 knowledgeImpact computes every link, narrative, architecture comparison, measurement and Sol assessment resting on a source, claim, release or pack. knowledgeNeighbourhood backlinks (public/knowledge-graph.js:70-80) list a focused node's direct edges. The SDD registers annex prints each object ← claim with source path, revision, lines and SHA-256 (public/knowledge-governance.js:254; public/review-domain.js:564). The full impact is shown only at withdrawal; see K-21.
- **Version pinning of product claims against the selected Chapter 7 version (knowledge-a items 67, 82, 85; knowledge-b items 61, 93)** — A product-mechanism claim must name a product and a documented version scope (public/architecture-knowledge-unit.js:61). That scope is matched against the option's product and version (public/architecture-facts.js:67-80; public/architecture-knowledge.js:29; public/product-choice.js:85), and Ch7 warns on a mismatch (public/technology-catalogue.js:58-63). Reviewed traits carry versionScope and accessedAt (public/product-knowledge.js:131).
- **Review dates for reusable guidance, and access dates on sources (knowledge-a items 42, 48, 54; knowledge-b items 50, 57, 66, 82, 84, 90-94)** — A project library entry's reviewAfter makes it non-current and raises 'Review changed reusable guidance' (public/guidance-record.js:18-19). An organisation entry's reviewDue blocks publishing an overdue entry and makes an installed one ineligible (organization-service.js:86, :145). AIW's own pack references carry accessedAt (public/composition-manifest.js:52; public/persistence-knowledge.js:9-12; public/read-design-knowledge.js:8-9). knowledge.source requires a 'revision or retrieval date' and records acquiredAt (public/knowledge-governance.js:135). Neither date applies to governed claims; see K-11.
- **Narrow 'does not establish' statements and access dates on built-in references (knowledge-a item 33)** — public/composition-knowledge.js:11-23: each inspected reference has a statement hash, accessedAt and a bounded statement such as 'A breaker contains a failure; it cannot make a missing business result successful'.
- **Non-guarantees of mechanisms stated and checked (knowledge-b items 6, 49)** — For execution contracts these are blockers: acceptance is not completion, a missing response is not failure, transport deduplication is not business protection, and a queue does not close the loss window (public/architecture-applicability.js:78-95). The built-in decision models carry similar rules (public/composition-knowledge.js:113-121). Outside these, see K-13.
- **Catalogue entries treated as reference designs needing local qualification (knowledge-a item 68)** — The catalogue is descriptive, candidate packs are never read, and library content never scores (public/knowledge-structure.js:1-3; public/component-library.js:11). Organisation entries need a different reviewer, conditions, limitations, a signed release and a project install (organization-service.js:79-96). Unreviewed library entries can still be proposed; see K-05.
- **Consumer record of the pattern version, inputs and exceptions a project uses (knowledge-b items 11, 54)** — Partly. workspace.guidance records the entry revision, adaptation, owner and reason, and raises 'Review changed reusable guidance' (public/workbench-domain.js:68-74; public/guidance-record.js:19). An owner can list consuming projects for shared assets (organization-service.js:37-44). Pattern, standard and reference-architecture entries are not covered; see K-19.
- **Admission criteria for catalogue entries (knowledge-b item 71)** — Partly. A structured claim needs a mechanism, 1-12 effects, 1-12 obligations with the evidence that checks them, conditions, limitations and a licence note (public/architecture-knowledge-unit.js:56-76; public/knowledge-governance.js:49-53, :172, :204). Organisation approval requires conditions and limitations (organization-service.js:80). See K-12 for the editorial checklist.
- **Replacement links and stable identifiers in knowledge maintenance (knowledge-a item 87; knowledge-b item 52)** — Sources and releases carry supersedes (public/knowledge-governance.js:150, :219-230), and a superseded source makes its claims ineligible (:60). Organisation and project library entries keep revision history (organization-service.js:75; public/workbench-domain.js:187). There is no claim-level successor (K-12) and no Playbook-to-catalogue identity resolution (K-07).
- **Quality lenses must never become an automatic score (knowledge-b item 80)** — The magnitude of catalogue quality impacts is deliberately excluded (public/knowledge-structure.js:30-32), and receipts carry scoring:false (public/knowledge-governance.js:71).
- **Established practice kept distinct from editorial synthesis (knowledge-b items 88, 96)** — Record-level authority and lineage are kept: knowledgeAuthority, editorialSynthesisClaimCount, liveImmutableSourceClaimCount (public/knowledge-structure.js:20). Repository seed leads stay 'discovery-only' (public/knowledge-structure.js:55-64).
- **Principles recorded as knowledge (knowledge-b item 43)** — Partly. A governed claim can carry architectureUnit kind 'design-principle' (public/architecture-knowledge-unit.js:6) and be linked to a model object (public/knowledge-governance.js:242-245). No chapter reads it; see K-23.
