# Chapter 4 · Logical application

Part of the AIW handbook gap review (AIW-HANDBOOK-GAP-REVIEW-2026-10-30.md). Gaps verified against the AIW source (paths relative to AIW-V5-Local-Source) and challenged by a skeptic; handbook references are "Handbook › heading (section id)".


**How AIW compares with the handbooks.** AIW's Chapter 4 does the first half of the handbooks' logical-application method well. Every requirement must reach an authored responsibility. A responsibility can be confirmed only once it states its purpose, its boundary (written as exclusions), its owner and its source. Every journey step must be served, links are fingerprinted, and candidate allocations to components carry a role, a scope and evidence. The handbooks, however, treat this chapter as the place where boundaries are designed and justified: bounded contexts and the relations between them, consistency boundaries tested against invariants, candidate partitions compared by change coupling and validated with change, failure and ownership-transfer scenarios, and owners with decision rights. AIW holds none of these as first-class records. Responsibilities are drafted one per requirement or per requirement group, and grouped under a title and purpose only, so the decomposition has no recorded basis; the stage named 'Set ownership boundaries' does not even store an owner on the group. Some building blocks exist elsewhere: the structure and persistence design tasks block distributed atomicity, Model Alternatives compare group reassignments by finding delta, Chapter 3 alternatives can propose responsibility relationships, Chapter 8 records invariants and outcome authorities, and Chapter 5 has an advisory cycle detector. None of them is wired to Chapter 4 boundaries or checked there. Governed knowledge is effectively absent: the catalogue holds the domain-design patterns, but no Chapter 4 picker, library kind or reader uses them. The SDD prints group purposes and responsibility refs, not boundaries, owners, connections or rationale. The doctrine fits well (drafts are proposals, Sol never fills owners or approves, knowledge never scores), so every recommendation below can follow AIW's existing pattern: person-stated records, deterministic checks, and Sol proposals that a person accepts.

**What AIW already does well**

- **Requirement coverage computed as a union, through responsibility groups** — public/logical-domain.js:130-132 raises 'uncovered' (error) for every requirement not linked, directly or through a requirement group, to an authored (not proposed) responsibility.
- **Discipline in defining a responsibility** — public/logical-domain.js:135,183 require purpose, boundary, owner and source before confirming; the boundary help asks 'What is explicitly outside this responsibility, and who owns that instead' (public/record-fields.js:238); fields can be recorded as open questions rather than invented (public/logical-domain.js:32-44).
- **Fingerprinted traceability to requirements, drivers and decisions** — Responsibilities fingerprint their inputs and raise 'source-change' errors (public/logical-domain.js:140, :174-186); mappings go stale when the responsibility changes (public/logical-domain.js:156); drivers bear on a responsibility through links, decisions, shared requirements and groups (public/logical-domain.js:57-66).
- **Responsibilities anchored to the journey, with relationships typed by ArchiMate rules** — Every journey step needs a connected responsibility ('process-gap', public/logical-domain.js:151-153); each responsibility must reach the journey ('disconnected', :144); ArchiMate typing raises RELATION-INVALID (public/architecture-model.js:446-450).
- **Allocation semantics carried into Chapter 5** — A mapping records role (realises or supports), scope, owner and evidence, and 'reviewed' requires all three (public/logical-domain.js:24-28, :203); Chapter 5 flags overlapping allocation scopes and duplicate data ownership (public/realisation-domain.js:158).
- **Decisions shape the logical model under control** — Chapter 3 alternatives must name responsibilities (public/decisions-domain.js:125) and can propose up to 12 relationships (public/decisions-domain.js:25; public/record-fields.js:131), which become read-only Chapter 4 edges once applied (public/decisions-domain.js:244).
- **Boundary design tasks that block distributed atomicity** — The 'structure' topic refuses separate services unless the person declares that no single atomic transaction spans both responsibilities (public/composition-knowledge.js:85, :122); the persistence task blocks cross-store atomic scope (public/persistence-knowledge.js:49).
- **Governance doctrine kept** — Proposed records are excluded from checks (public/logical-domain.js:130); ghost proposals are deterministic and previewed before they are kept (public/logical-domain.js:214-226); field drafts never fill owner, source or evidence (public/field-suggestions.js:105).

### C4-01 · No bounded-context or context-map record: semantic boundaries, relationship patterns and translation ownership cannot be recorded  
*Severity high · effort L*

- **Handbooks:** Inventory bounded contexts, the scopes within which one model is coherent, with accountable owners. Label each pair of contexts with a relationship type (partnership, customer/supplier, conformist, protective translation or ACL, shared kernel), keeping upstream/downstream model influence separate from request or data direction, and name the translation owner and the compatibility obligation. Keep current and target maps. A shared kernel needs a scope exclusion list, named joint owners and a joint change protocol. A context need not equal a service, team or deployment.  
  _Refs:_ Application Architecture Handbook › Context Mapping (APP-MD-011); Shared Kernel (APP-MD-012) › Forces and mechanism; Scoped technical parameters; Variants (Application_Architecture_Handbook__02); Application Architecture Handbook › Domain modelling and decomposition › APP-MD-001 Bounded Context (Application_Architecture_Handbook__01); Application Architecture Handbook › Comparative design essays › DD-02 Distinguish model meaning, invariants, services, and deployment (Application_Architecture_Handbook__09); Business Architecture Handbook › BIZ-XD-05, BIZ-XD-06, BIZ-XD-12 › Compose the domain mechanisms (Business_Architecture_Handbook__04); Application Architecture Handbook › Failure patterns and remedies › APP-AP-012 Universal Domain Model Monopoly › Forces and mechanism (Application_Architecture_Handbook__08); Data Architecture Handbook › DAT-ST-005 Domain-oriented Data Products (Data_Architecture_Handbook__01)
- **AIW today:** A Chapter 4 group stores a title and a purpose; only the API and the drafter can add up to 12 module links. A connection has one of four kinds (structural, flow, serves, realises), with a label and a condition. There is no context record, no relation pattern, no upstream/downstream influence, no translation owner and no shared-kernel scope. Bounded Context, Context Map, Shared Kernel, Conformist, Customer-Supplier and Anti-Corruption Layer exist only as AKR catalogue records tagged logicalApplication. No Chapter 4 code reads them, and they can be typed only as free text in a Chapter 3 alternative's pattern field. Chapter 8 parties have a current, target or retiring status and free text on facilitation, nothing more.  
  _Evidence:_ public/record-fields.js:137 — logical.group has only title and purpose; public/logical-domain.js:36 — CONNECTION_KINDS are trace, flow, serves and realises only; public/logical-domain.js:200 — the group command accepts title, purpose and module links (requirementGroupIds) only; public/logical-ui.js:64 — the group form sends title and purpose; public/brain-catalogue.js:64,88,125,175,289,295 — PAT-CONTEXT-MAP, PAT-SHARED-KERNEL, PAT-BOUNDED-CONTEXT, PAT-CONFORMIST-CONTEXT, PAT-ANTI-CORRUPTION-LAYER and PAT-CUSTOMER-SUPPLIER-CONTEXT exist as catalogue data only; no reader of lifecycleStages was found; public/record-fields.js:431-434 — catalogue pattern names appear only as a datalist for the Chapter 3 alternative; public/interfaces-domain.js:40 — party status is current, target or retiring
- **Why it matters:** Without contexts and typed relations, an architect cannot show where a term changes meaning, who adapts when an upstream model changes, or where an ACL or a shared kernel is needed. Modules (functional decomposition) and groups are then read as semantic boundaries, which the handbooks name as a failure pattern (a universal domain model).
- **Recommendation:** Add a logical.context record to the Groups stage, or a 'context' kind on logical.group. Fields: title, model scope, accountable model owner (C4-04), member responsibilities and groups, key terms with their meaning, explicit exclusions, and current or target plateau through the existing review.plateau-assign. Add logical.contextRelation: from and to context; pattern (partnership, customer-supplier, conformist, anti-corruption layer, open host or published language, shared kernel, separate ways); the model-influence (upstream) side, kept separate from the data direction of Chapter 5 interactions and Chapter 8 contracts; translation owner; translating responsibility id; compatibility obligation. For a shared kernel add an inclusion list, an exclusion list, at least two joint owners and a joint change protocol. Validate checks: an ACL relation must name a translating responsibility that a component realises; a shared kernel needs two or more owners and an exclusion list; a Chapter 8 contract between components that realise responsibilities in different contexts with no relation raises a warning; a context with no owner raises a warning. Add a Model view 'Context map' (contexts as nodes, relation-pattern labels, upstream marks, current/target filter) and an SDD section 4 'Context map' passage. Offer the released AKR domain patterns as descriptive guidance on the relation form, with receipts, never scored. Sol may propose contexts from conflicting term usage across requirements and data definitions, as proposals a person keeps or dismisses.
- **Depends on:** C4-04, deep-dive library-coverage: Catalogue patterns cannot be attached to records outside Chapter 3 free text and Chapter 5's 19 Playbook ids; Chapters 9 and 10 have no picker

### C4-02 · Boundaries are not derived from shared rules, change reasons and authority, nor chosen among candidate partitions  
*Severity high · effort L*

- **Handbooks:** Discover boundaries by interviewing about concrete decisions. Group responsibilities that use the same rules and change for the same business reason. Treat invariants that need one authority as hard constraints, then compare a few candidate partitions by change-coupling evidence over declared interaction layers, never summing unlike units, and by a consequential change, to separate accidental from essential coupling. Keep capabilities, semantic domains, obligations and implementations as separate many-to-many views, and justify any one-to-one mapping between semantic, invariant, contract and deployment boundaries.  
  _Refs:_ Application Architecture Master Handbook > Chapter 02 > Discover boundaries through language and change; Map responsibilities into modules; Compare boundary options through a consequential change; AAP-S002 (Application_Architecture_Master__01); Enterprise Architecture Handbook › EA-SB-003 Capability and Domain Boundary Design › Forces and mechanism / Technical depth / Conditional design choices (Enterprise_Architecture_Handbook__01); Software Application Design Handbook › SAD 05 › Discover boundaries through language and change / Map responsibilities into modules; SAD 06 › Define module boundaries that can survive growth (Software_Application_Design_Handbook__01); Application Architecture Handbook › Comparative design essays › DD-02 Distinguish model meaning, invariants, services, and deployment; Learning paths › Separate semantic and runtime boundaries (Application_Architecture_Handbook__09)
- **AIW today:** The Chapter 4 drafters create one responsibility per requirement (journey drafter), or one per requirement group with one group per module (architecture drafter). A person then places responsibilities in groups with no recorded basis. Partial means exist. Model Alternatives can reassign groupId for up to 12 existing responsibilities and compare finding deltas, but cannot create groups. A Chapter 3 decision's alternatives can propose relationships between responsibilities. The 'structure' composition topic compares a modular layered application with separate services, but for two responsibilities typed as prose, and it creates new responsibilities. The Chapter 4 workbench offers only a vocabulary-overlap review and lists no library kinds. Chapter 1 business rules link requirements and Chapter 8 data, but Chapter 4 never reads them, so 'these responsibilities share the same rules' cannot be shown.  
  _Evidence:_ public/journey-drafter.js:319-324 — Chapter 4 drafting is one responsibility per requirement, named from its title; public/architecture-drafter.js:31-33 — one responsibility per requirement group, and one responsibility group per module; public/model-alternatives.js:18-21 — alternatives edit 1-12 existing Chapter 4-7 records only; public/model-impact.js:15 — a responsibility edit can change groupId; public/composition-knowledge.js:81-88 — the structure topic takes firstResponsibility and secondResponsibility as prose; public/composition-design.js:31 — composition creates new '<role> · REQ · DES' responsibilities; public/architecture-analysis.js:64-69 — Chapter 4 analysis is word overlap between responsibilities; public/decisions-domain.js:125,244 — alternatives name responsibilities; their applied relationships become Chapter 4 edges; public/requirements-domain.js:178,210-213 — business rules link requirements and data; no Chapter 4 reader; public/workbench-ui.js:103 — no library kinds are relevant to Chapter 4
- **Why it matters:** The decomposition is the main artefact of Chapter 4. Without a recorded basis and rejected alternatives, a reviewer cannot tell a semantic boundary from a copied org chart or a requirement list, and Chapters 5 to 10 inherit an unjustified partition.
- **Recommendation:** Add a 'Derive boundaries' step to the Groups stage, in four parts. (1) Deterministic signals for each pair of responsibilities, shown and never scored: shared Chapter 1 business rules (through their requirements), shared Chapter 8 invariants or data authority, shared accepted decisions, shared drivers, and the number of Chapter 4 connections. (2) A logical.partition candidate (2-4 per project or module) that assigns responsibilities to groups or contexts, implemented by extending Model Alternatives so a bundle can create groups. (3) A comparison view showing each candidate's counts per declared interaction layer side by side, never summed: cross-group connections, cross-group invariants, cross-group shared rules and implied contracts, plus the C4-05 scenario results. (4) The choice recorded by a person as a Chapter 3 decision of topic 'decomposition', whose alternatives are the partitions (responsibilityIds and proposed relationships are already supported), with reasons for rejecting the others. Validate: warn when a project has more than one group but no current decomposition decision covers them; warn when a group's members share no rule, invariant or decision and its purpose is open. Let the 'structure' topic choose two existing responsibilities instead of prose. Sol may propose candidate partitions and pairwise rationale, with receipts, and never selects.
- **Depends on:** C4-03, C4-05, deep-dive component-selection: Derivation is mechanical: one component per responsibility, not derived from obligations and drivers, deep-dive component-selection: Composition and guided tasks create new responsibilities instead of realising the existing Chapter 4 ones

### C4-03 · An invariant split across components, groups or contexts is never detected, and Chapter 4 has no consistency boundary  
*Severity high · effort M*

- **Handbooks:** Before splitting into services, schemas or shards, check whether an invariant would span the proposed boundary, and keep tightly atomic rules within one viable boundary: asynchronous events alone do not enforce a shared credit limit or a uniqueness rule. ### C4-07 · No allowed or forbidden dependency rules between groups or layers, no private-data rule, and no architecture-test obligation

### C4-16 · The Responsibilities view is shaped like a service blueprint but has no frontstage/backstage layers, channel, per-interaction owner or recovery route  
*Severity low · effort S*

- **Handbooks:** A blueprint separates customer actions, visible interactions, backstage work and supporting services. Each critical interaction gets an owner, an information requirement and a recovery route. Case identity is preserved across channels, the context passed at a handoff is purpose-limited and fresh, and the burden transferred to the customer is measured.  
  _Refs:_ Business Architecture Handbook › BIZ-VS-003 Journey and Service-blueprint Alignment (Business_Architecture_Handbook__01)
- **AIW today:** The Responsibilities view lays journey steps across and responsibility groups down, with 'serves the journey step' connections. There is no layer attribute (customer action, frontstage, backstage, support), no channel, no per-interaction owner or recovery route, and no measure of customer effort.  
  _Evidence:_ public/responsibility-model.js:1-8 — laid out 'the way a service blueprint does'; public/instrument-tray.js:55-58 — the Chapter 4 views are Responsibilities and Coverage; public/logical-domain.js:36 — a 'serves' connection has a label and condition only
- **Why it matters:** Without the visibility lines and a recovery route per interaction, the view cannot show where a customer-facing failure lands or who recovers it.
- **Recommendation:** Add an optional blueprint layer to responsibilities (customer action, frontstage interaction, backstage work, support service), defaulted from the responsibility kind (a channel kind becomes frontstage), and draw the lines of interaction and visibility in the Responsibilities view. Add a channel and a recovery route to 'serves' connections on critical steps. Warn when a step served by a frontstage responsibility has no recovery route. The customer-effort measure stays a Chapter 2 driver.
