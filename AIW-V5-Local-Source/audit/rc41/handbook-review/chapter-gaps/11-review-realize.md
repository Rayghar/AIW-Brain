# Chapter 11 · Review & realize

Part of the AIW handbook gap review (AIW-HANDBOOK-GAP-REVIEW-2026-10-30.md). Gaps verified against the AIW source (paths relative to AIW-V5-Local-Source) and challenged by a skeptic; handbook references are "Handbook › heading (section id)".


**How AIW compares with the handbooks.** Across the three handbook families, Chapter 11 is the stage where the architect tries to break the design, turns it into a qualified and operated service, and later retires it. AIW follows this stance closely on honesty and authority. Rules raise findings, and a person must treat each one with evidence. Typed checks are bound to an accepted obligation, the exact objects, an environment, a release and a source revision. They read fail before unknown before pass, and they go stale when their basis changes. Review, approval and handoff are person-only commands, approval needs a second signed-in person, and every output disclaims production readiness. The weaknesses fall in four places. In governance, one reviewer can treat a hard-constraint breach away as an accepted limitation; limitations and approval conditions are untracked free text that never expires; verification has no separation of duties; a failing check blocks nothing; and recording evidence even makes the approved baseline stale. In the assurance argument, AIW has no claim register, evidence ladder, verification-case record with an oracle and a negative control, conformance population or reviewer-raised finding, and patterns adopted outside design tasks bring no validation scenarios. In transition and after delivery, plateaus record only which design objects exist, while the handbooks model how authority, cohorts, contract versions, reconciliation, reversal options, rehearsals, residual obligations, retirement, incidents and realised benefits move and feed back into decisions; AIW holds most of this as eight prose migration fields, or not at all. Many items reinforce gaps the deep-dives already confirmed (decommissioning, fitness functions, waivers, test strategy, operational acceptance, RAID), so they are cited as dependencies rather than repeated, and the six retirement items from four handbook families suggest the decommissioning dive deserves high severity.

### C11-01 · Hard constraints and mandatory invariants can be treated away: no non-waivable finding class and no mandatory-gate reading  
*Severity high · effort M*

- **Handbooks:** Readiness rests on a small set of mandatory gates: financial integrity, authorization, external outcome handling, recoverability, operational ownership and applicable obligations. Each gate is satisfied, blocked or explicitly outside the launch scope, unknown fails closed, and no score or set of passing checks offsets a failed hard gate. Waiving a preference is not the same as permitting an invariant violation: hard invariants and binding obligations cannot be waived, an exception never turns absent evidence into PASS, and the evaluator is never quietly changed to let a release pass; a reviewed decision changes the requirement or scope instead.

### C11-03 · Approval conditions and approved scope are untracked free text: there is no scoped release recommendation with enforceable conditions  
*Severity high · effort M*

- **Handbooks:** A release decision states exactly what is approved, for which candidate and scope (for example continued implementation and target qualification, but not production activation).
