# Chapter 7 · Technology realization (products)

Part of the AIW handbook gap review (AIW-HANDBOOK-GAP-REVIEW-2026-10-30.md). Gaps verified against the AIW source (paths relative to AIW-V5-Local-Source) and challenged by a skeptic; handbook references are "Handbook › heading (section id)".


**How AIW compares with the handbooks.** Chapter 7 follows the handbooks' basic order. A vendor-neutral Chapter 6 capability is realised by comparing at least two concrete options (product, version, tier, vendor, operating model, typed configuration facts). The comparison runs against inherited drivers, four fixed criteria, requirement groups and accepted design-task obligations. An option that breaks a hard constraint is excluded, only a person can record the selection, the selection goes stale when its inputs change, and approval evidence is kept separate. Product mechanisms count only when a version and matching configuration facts are recorded. Assessments go stale when the option's version or configuration changes. Both rules match the handbooks' insistence that behaviour is qualified for the selected configuration, not the product label. What is missing is most of what the handbooks put around each option:
- an explicit boundary of what the product does not guarantee, turned into a residual obligation elsewhere;
- a capability matrix derived from Chapter 8 contracts and adopted patterns, with selected values checked against required values;
- sourcing shapes (build, reuse, buy, managed, SaaS) with a fit assessment;
- the organisation's demonstrated operating capability as a criterion and as a condition on the selection;
- a per-option cost model built from cost drivers, with cost per useful outcome and switching points.
Option facts are author-entered and undated. Options have no region, quota or service-contract terms, no pre-selection experiment record and no exit feasibility test. The SDD body prints only capability → product with a selection label, so the comparison, the rejected options and the conditions appear only in the register annex. The deep dives already cover self-approval, recording without assessments or gates, licence and lifecycle data, standards conformance, target binding, qualification state, shared responsibility and review triggers. The gaps below add the remaining method steps.

### C7-02 · Required behavioural capabilities are not derived into a capability matrix, and Chapter 8 contract semantics never feed back into the Chapter 7 selection  
*Severity high · effort L*

- **Handbooks:** Write the invariant record, the boundary table and the required contract behaviour before selecting products. Then qualify products and partners against that behaviour: durability, ordering, redelivery, idempotency scope and retention, authoritative outcome query, replay horizon. Keep a versioned capability matrix with columns requirement, claim, selected value, evidence, limitation, owner and review trigger. Blank cells are never defaults. A selected setting can still violate the requirement (idempotency retention shorter than the recovery window). Equal method names or a new product label do not establish substitutability.  
  _Refs:_ Enterprise Architecture Handbook › Pattern composition and compatibility › EA-PC-001 Implementation; EA-PC-004 Technical depth, Falsification and Worked example; EA-QA-004 Implementation (Enterprise_Architecture_Handbook__02); Integration Architecture Vol 7 › INT-AD-003 Anti Corruption Layer › Technical deep dive › Mechanics, Parameters (Capability matrix version) (Integration_Architecture_Volume_7_Handbook__02); Integration Architecture Volume 7 Handbook › INT-PT-003 Partner Onboarding and Capability Negotiation › Mechanism and structure; Technical deep dive › Mechanics (Integration_Architecture_Volume_7_Handbook__04); Application Architecture Handbook › Composition recipes › APP-RC-008 Provider migration; Comparative design essays › DD-03, DD-05 (Application_Architecture_Handbook__09); Quality Architecture Volume 9 › QAE-CI-002; QAE-CI-003; QAE-CI-006 › Technical depth (Quality_Architecture_Volume_9_Handbook__02); Quality Architecture Volume 9 › QAE-DP-001 › Implementation approach; Parameters and consequences (Quality_Architecture_Volume_9_Handbook__03); Applied Solution Architecture Vol 10 › FND-03; Chapter 4 › Decision map (polling vs CDC outbox) (Applied_Solution_Architecture_Volume_10_Handbook__01); Enterprise Component Library Part 1 › CL-FND-05 Choose technologies using the required semantics › Compare capabilities before products (Enterprise_Component_Library_Part_1_Application_and_Resilience__01); Software Application Design Handbook › SAD 15 › Select a delivery design; SAD 16 › Choose orchestration and compensation deliberately (Software_Application_Design_Handbook__01); Target Platform Qualification Handbook › TQ C02 › Replacing the fixture with an enterprise identity adapter (Target_Platform_Qualification_and_Operational_Acceptance_Handbook__01)
- **AIW today:** Derivation is partial. Accepted obligations from applied Companion design tasks become Ch7 criteria (weight 0, but each must be assessed), and configuration facts encode a few semantic prerequisites (atomic key and effect, Kafka retention, RabbitMQ manual acknowledgement) for 9 products. Chapter 8 never contributes:
- the input fingerprint covers capabilities, boundaries, dependencies, applications, requirements, drivers, decisions, groups, obligations and knowledge, but not contracts or data. A Ch8 duplicate policy, timeout or retentionDays recorded later therefore adds no criterion and does not stale the selection. Ch8 depends on Ch7 only the other way, through 'technology-choice'.
- An assessment is an effect or coverage judgement, with no required value, claimed value, selected setting, limitation or owner.
- Ch8 parties have no capability profile.
- Changing an option's product only flags its assessments for review; there is no behavioural equivalence comparison.  
  _Evidence:_ public/technology-realisation-domain.js:56-57 — criteria: drivers, four fixed, requirement groups, obligations (weight 0); public/architecture-obligations.js:64-70 — obligations come only from applied design-task solutions; public/technology-realisation-domain.js:79 — inputSnapshot has no Ch8 contract, data or party stamps; public/interfaces-domain.js:312 — only the reverse dependency exists ('technology-choice'); public/record-fields.js:165,171 — Ch8 idempotencyKey, duplicatePolicy, retryPolicy, timeoutMs and data retentionDays, none read by Ch7; public/record-fields.js:173 — interfaces.party has no capability fields; public/technology-realisation-domain.js:197 — a product change only sets reviewRequired on assessments; public/product-knowledge.js:57,84,89 — semantic facts exist only for bundled product families
- **Why it matters:** Products are selected in Ch7 before the Ch8 contract fixes the behaviour they must provide. AIW can therefore carry a Kafka retention shorter than the replay horizon, or a provider without an outcome enquiry, as a current selection with no finding. That is exactly the failure the handbooks' capability-negotiation method exists to prevent.
- **Recommendation:** Add 'required capability' rows on techrealisation.plan: {capability (deduplication, ordered delivery per key, replay horizon, outcome enquiry, isolation level, cancellation window, token validation profile), required value, unit, source record}. The source record is a Ch8 contract or data definition, a Ch5 interaction, an accepted obligation or a Ch9 control parameter.
- Proposing rows: deterministically from the Ch8 contracts and data where the realization participates (idempotencyKey + duplicatePolicy → deduplication with retention ≥ recovery window; retentionDays → retention/replay; timeoutMs → acknowledgement window) and from accepted obligations. Sol may propose more rows with citations; a person keeps them.
- Matrix: per option, a cell {claimed value (vendor or partner), selected setting, evidence level (declared / selected / demonstrated / observed), limitation, owner}.
- Validate:
  - a blank cell on the preferred option → 'to confirm', never a default;
  - a selected numeric setting weaker than the required value → error;
  - a gateway or adapter is never credited with a capability the downstream provider lacks.
- Add the stamps of participating Ch8 contracts and data to inputSnapshot, so a contract change stales the selection.
- Give Ch8 external parties the same matrix; a missing mandatory capability raises 'partner-capability'.
- When an option's product changes, show the matrix diff and require a reviewed equivalence note before re-recording.
- Output: a capability × option matrix in Model › Options, and the SDD prints the selected option's matrix.
- Doctrine: candidate claims never fill cells.
- **Depends on:** deep-dive technology-selection: No qualification or operational-acceptance state for a selected technology, deep-dive technology-selection: No provider-neutral attribute schema or fitness tests per capability type, deep-dive data-and-integration: Partial: chapter handoffs drop data and scheduling facts

### C7-04 · The organisation's operating capability is not recorded or assessed, and a selection cannot be made conditional on it  
*Severity high · effort M*

- **Handbooks:** 'Technology selection should follow the people who can safely operate it.' Inventory the inherited platform support with evidence: on-call role, node operating mode, image maintenance, upgrade process, incident access, artifact registry. Verify capability by having the platform owner perform a cold deployment, credential rotation, rollback, node or task replacement, log retrieval and restore handoff, recording human effort. Choose for example EKS only when that capability is shown; otherwise a simpler managed runtime remains the credible candidate. The selection is conditional, and loss of team support is a review trigger.

- **AIW today:** Operational ownership is a fixed criterion weighing 1. Its deterministic suggestion reads only the operating model: every self-managed option gets a 'trade-off' and every provider-managed option 'supports', whatever platform the organisation already runs. That is the opposite of the handbooks' EKS reasoning when a supported Kubernetes platform exists. Ch3 'skills' is free text. - **Why it matters:** Eight handbooks make operating capability the deciding factor between runtime platforms. AIW's only automated hint points the other way, and a choice justified by 'we already run it' cannot be recorded with its evidence or become stale when that stops being true.
- **Recommendation:** Add an 'operating capability' record, either project-local or released through the four-eyes organisation library: technology or platform, owning team, on-call coverage, skills evidence, and exercises (cold deploy, credential rotation, rollback, node or task replacement, log retrieval, restore handoff). - Replace the blanket operatingTrait suggestion with a reading of the record: demonstrated → supports; absent for a self-managed option → trade-off; claimed → needs evidence.
- Let a recorded selection carry conditions ('holds while CAP-x is demonstrated'), and include the capability's stamp in inputSnapshot so a lapsed or retired capability stales the selection.

- Sol may draft the exercise checklist; only a person records results.
- The SDD prints the capability assumption and the condition with the selection.
