# Chapter 6 · Logical technology

Part of the AIW handbook gap review (AIW-HANDBOOK-GAP-REVIEW-2026-10-30.md). Gaps verified against the AIW source (paths relative to AIW-V5-Local-Source) and challenged by a skeptic; handbook references are "Handbook › heading (section id)".


**How AIW compares with the handbooks.** Chapter 6 covers the first half of the method these handbooks prescribe. Every component need must map to a vendor-neutral capability. Each capability states its purpose, the boundary between platform and application, its owner, rationale, trust boundary and failure domain. Continuity is declared and checked as fail-closed (bypass, redundant, single), stateful categories need recovery and backup links, and a four-step failure lab propagates loss along critical dependencies. Where a capability comes from one of the rule-based design tasks (interaction, publication, resilience, traffic/edge, partitioning, structure, persistence, reads), it is derived from a recorded Chapter 3 decision with alternatives and applicability gates. The method the handbooks centre on, a failure model rather than a list of components, is only partly supported. Failure domains are free-text strings matched by string comparison, there is one fault per simulation and outage is binary. There is no serving/control/recovery plane distinction, no failure-mode worksheet, no named degraded modes per business operation and no availability composition, and external providers stay outside the dependency graph. Selecting a capability is mostly a pick from 15 closed categories filled from templates. There is no selection path with fit criteria and alternatives for most capability types, and no library or reference-architecture route into Chapter 6. Shared platform capabilities have no provider/consumer contract. Chapter 6's engineering content (dependencies, failure domains, continuity, trust boundaries) reaches the SDD only as annex registers and one figure.

**What AIW already does well**

- **Need-driven derivation of capabilities with full support coverage** — Every component must have needs ('no-needs' E), every kept need must map to a capability ('unsupported' E), mapping scope is required and a category mismatch warns (public/technology-domain.js:215-225). Need wording is derived from the drivers whose design questions the category answers (public/technology-domain.js:168-184). A recorded need is never rewritten; the new wording is offered as an edit the person accepts (public/technology-ui.js:62-63).
- **Capabilities derived from recorded decisions in rule-based design tasks** — The composition, persistence, read and interaction design tasks write every option as a Chapter 3 alternative, then create or reuse a Chapter 6 capability with decisionIds and driverIds, status candidate (public/composition-design.js:66-79; public/persistence-design.js:57-63; public/read-design.js:49-53; public/architecture-design.js:164-168). Options carry applicability gates such as 'freshness must be bounded' for cache-aside (public/read-design-knowledge.js:19-24).
- **Fail-closed continuity rules and essentiality propagation** — Essentiality propagates down critical dependency and trust links. 'single-point' warns when an essential need rests on one path, 'isolation' fires when a redundant capability has no distinct alternate domain or plan, and 'unsafe-bypass' marks identity and transactional bypass as an error ('must not fail open') (public/technology-domain.js:228,239-242).
- **Recovery obligations on stateful and added categories** — Transactional and messaging capabilities need a recovery plan, owner, RTO and RPO, plus resilience links to backup and recovery capabilities. Scheduling, workflow, search, reporting and archive each have their own check (public/technology-domain.js:243-256).
- **Failure lab that keeps unknowns honest** — The simulation propagates loss along critical links. An application takes the worst consequence its needs state, and an unstated consequence stays 'not stated', never assumed degraded. Assumed driver values need a basis, and the result is recorded only after the person walks all four steps (public/technology-domain.js:381-410,421). The export banner says outcomes 'are assumptions, not runtime evidence' (public/technology-domain.js:439).
- **Platform model insights** — The Platform view computes blast radius per capability and per failure domain, and flags 'all N capabilities share one failure domain', wide blast radius, critical chains and single paths (public/platform-model.js:35-40,202-210).
- **Doctrine encoded in category templates** — The templates carry rules such as 'Never use a cache-only check to authorise a financial effect', 'A successful copy is not proof of a successful restore' and 'without treating an unavailable control as permission' (public/technology-domain.js:107-124).
- **Change detection and person-only acceptance** — capabilitySnapshot fingerprints needs, applications, drivers and decisions, and raises 'source-change' (public/technology-domain.js:136-138,232). technology.proposal refuses unless reviewed:true (public/technology-domain.js:419). Recovery numbers stay assumptions until targetsConfirmed (public/technology-domain.js:257).
- **Alternative capability arrangements can be saved and compared** — Coordinated model alternatives accept technology.capability edits (public/alternative-editor.js:16-17; public/model-alternatives.js:21). They are compared on diff, capacity and cost, and a person records accepted or not selected with a reason (public/workbench-domain.js:51-58,166-172).

### C6-01 · Failure domains are free-text strings: no shared-cause register, nominated fault set, correlated loss or independence evidence  
*Severity high · effort L*

- **Handbooks:** Build a failure model rather than only a deployment diagram. Annotate dependencies on power, network path, naming, identity, key service, configuration authority, release pipeline, operators and carriers, and nominate the fault set, because independence is specific to a cause and a time horizon. Identify minimal cut sets, including logical and administrative common causes. Two carrier names, two node pools or two regions are not proof of independence; require shared-risk evidence, and fail a claimed independent site unless the journey works with primary identity, network and configuration blocked. Weigh concentration by the share of the workload exposed.

They are compared lowercased in 'isolation' and the simulation but case-sensitively in 'shared-recovery-domain'. A domain run fails one string-matched domain, so a capability cannot belong to several cause domains, and correlated or multi-domain loss cannot be simulated. Accepting the isolate proposal's placeholder clears 'isolation'. Logical common causes are only partly expressible. Insights count components lost, not obligations exposed.

### C6-02 · No serving/control/recovery plane distinction, autonomy horizon, static-stability or recovery-path independence check  
*Severity high · effort L*

- **Handbooks:** Separate the path that serves existing requests from the controls that create, change or repair it. State how long service continues without the control plane, bounded by cached configuration, certificates, leases and policy snapshots, and what happens at the earliest expiry. For each action, state the behaviour when control data is stale. Recovery dependencies (management access, identity, certificates, key access, resource creation) must work while the failed domain is unavailable: prefer pre-provisioned surviving capacity, test with primary management access blocked, and keep a recovery-dependency inventory with evidence (restore test, controlled decrypt test, configuration, secrets, DNS, images, procedures).

Chapter 10 holds a partial static-stability reading. Add a failure-lab mode 'control plane lost' that keeps serving capabilities available until the earliest stated expiry, labelled an assumption. Sol may draft autonomy rows as proposals that a person keeps.

### C6-05 · Alternate and bypass paths are not qualified: no AND/OR dependency semantics, validity conditions, fallback eligibility, outage-scale capacity, exercise recency or return to primary  
*Severity high · effort M*

- **Handbooks:** Model each realisation as a complete path that produces the outcome. Label dependencies required, conditional or substitutable, and state when an alternative is valid. Fallback is a different decision from failover to an equivalent replica. State the eligible error classes (never authorisation denials), the altered semantics and freshness (advisory results typed so they cannot authorise effects), capacity tested at outage-scale demand (800 req/s redirected onto a service tested at 80 req/s), independence from the primary's dependencies, an exercise cadence and return-to-primary reconciliation.

### C6-08 · No availability objective per capability and no composition along critical dependency chains (serial dependencies, common cause)  
*Severity high · effort M*

- **Handbooks:** A journey that synchronously requires several stores multiplies their availabilities (0.999^4 is about 99.60%, not 99.9%). Remove optional derivatives from the critical path. Count a common cause once and keep its floor: independent parallel failure of 0.0001 becomes about 0.0021 with common cause. A required serial dependency can dominate journey availability, so count failures as joint sets rather than multiplying availabilities when independence is not justified.  
  _Refs:_ Data Architecture Handbook › Polyglot Persistence (DAT-PC-006) › Worked technical reasoning; Scoped technical parameters (Data_Architecture_Handbook__02); Quality Architecture Volume 9 › QAE-LAB-11 Redundancy with explicit common-cause failure; QAE-EC-002 › Parameters and consequences (Quality_Architecture_Volume_9_Handbook__03); Quality Architecture Vol 9 › QAE-RC-001 Journey reliability and dependency exposure (Quality_Architecture_Volume_9_Handbook__01); Business Architecture Handbook › BIZ-CM-002 › Technical depth (Business_Architecture_Handbook__01)

It has no availability objective or target rows, unlike the Chapter 7 and Chapter 10 driver-linked target rows. Availability budgets are computed per Chapter 10 part from placements and recovery minutes, and latency is split per synchronous hop. No code composes availability across the Chapter 6 critical dependency chain, or counts a shared domain once with a floor; a search for serial or composite availability logic finds nothing.  
  _Evidence:_ public/record-fields.js:148 — capability numbers are recoveryMinutes and lossMinutes only; public/design-reasoning.js:267-290 — availabilityBudget and availabilityCheck per placed part; public/platform-model.js:206-208 — the critical chain is reported as text, with no arithmetic
- **Why it matters:** An availability driver can only be shown met when the chain of synchronous prerequisites beneath an operation is composed. Otherwise each capability looks fine on its own while the journey falls short of target, and redundancy sharing a cause is overcounted.

### C6-03 · No failure-mode analysis beyond binary outage: no slow, stale, duplicated or partial modes, no detection, containment or residual obligations  
*Severity medium · effort M*

- **Handbooks:** For each dependency and state transition on a critical journey, examine omission, delay, repetition, staleness, reordering, unauthorised action, incorrect result and partial completion, including just before and just after each durable effect.
