## A. Shared registration (S0)

**A1. `hard` is the rc.10.73.42 non-waivable class, and nothing stricter.**
A finding a package marks `hard` (level `error`) cannot be waived: it is not accepted as a design limitation, not
marked not applicable and not marked source-silent. Like a hard-constraint or invariant break, it is fixed before
review or carried as an action. There is one non-waivable class with one meaning.
- This corrects the plan's test 3, which said "a hard finding takes no disposition". Corrected: "a hard finding takes
  no waiver". Reason: wp2 and wp3 both ask for the rc.10.73.42 class; no design asks for a stricter one; and a finding
  with no treatment at all leaves a reviewer unable to record "fix before review".
- The suite's case 3 is replaced, recorded old → new: the three waivers are refused in words, the dialog offers the
  other treatments and no waiver, and the finding blocks exactly as a built-in non-waivable finding does.
- The words must fit any package: "“<title>” cannot be waived: change the design. Until then, fix it before review or
  carry it as an action."

**A2. Package ids are assigned by the lead** *(amended the same day by D8: the ids below replace the first list, which
ordered the packages by work-package number)*. An id orders a package's layers, findings, sections and upgrade step,
and a hop may come only from a package earlier by id. A route stub is `<id>-route`.

**A3. A package is held in an existing lazy entry.** S0 measured a lazy entry of its own at 0.8 to 1.2 KB on the
chapters route and two script files. No package gets its own entry without a grant from the lead, given per package
against a measurement.

**A4. Route allowances after S0** (4,243 B left of the unchanged cap 3,233,792 B):

Before wave 5 the lead recovers headroom by moving on-route code that only one lazily loaded surface uses off the

**A5. The second-person sentence stays `verifierRefusal`'s in S0.** A package's own surface says who may take the

**A6. Deferred, with an owner.** A package's records in a chapter's record count, and their types for relationships a

read comments.

## B. Knowledge forms and the concept register (wp1 S1)

The reviewer's two must-fix items and eleven should-fix items are all to be done. On the design concerns:

**B1. A claim that carries a form is read only by the concept readers.**
`releasedClaims` and every reader built on it or beside it do not receive a form claim in any state, released and
active included: knowledge structures, the architecture knowledge snapshot, architecture intelligence, the Brain
context, Sol's intelligence and reasoning packets, the product comparison, the stewardship reading. Decision-bearing
content reaches a design record only through an active pin; design section b.4 stands as written.
- Exclude at the fewest places that cover every reader. Up to 120 B of wp1's route allowance may be used.
- Case S1.10, released and active: every reading the reviewer listed (27) is asserted not to move, and the marker
  reaches none. State in the notes which readings a released, active form does move (the concept readers and the
  knowledge workspace's own lists and stamps).

**B2. Concepts and forms are written in a library project.**
`knowledge.concept`, `knowledge.topic`, and a `knowledge.claim` that carries a form are refused in a project that a
person has not marked as a knowledge library. Plain claims are unchanged everywhere.
- A project that holds a concept, a topic or a form claim cannot be unmarked.
- `conceptTeachingForms` returns unreleased content only in a library project. In any other project it returns
  released content only (plan section 6 item 4, until the owner decides).
- The inertness case still proves that a document which holds candidate forms anyway (written into the document, as a
  recovery or an older library might leave it) moves no design reading.

**B3. Only a person changes what a review covered.**
- `conceptHash` covers every field of the concept record except bookkeeping (`at`, `actor`, `lastEditor`, `history`,
  `revision`, `changeNote`, the review and the retirement). So `reviewDue`, `reviewTriggers`, `steward`, `notAdopted`,
  `successorOf` and `splitFrom` are in it. It also covers the sorted topic rows mapped to the concept (file, topic id,
  slice hash).
- A request written by an agent account, as a draft by Sol, as an import, or stating no author is refused when it
  would change the hash of a concept whose review stands or which is in a release. That includes adding a form claim
  to it and remapping a topic to or from it. On a draft concept the same request is accepted.
- A person's edit is accepted and lapses the review, as now.
- An upsert that changes nothing writes nothing: no history line, no new time, no new actor (concept and topic).

**B4. A concept review records the reviewer's account kind**, and the read-time check requires a person's. A review
without it does not stand.

**B5. Fail closed.** `conceptDecisionForms` returns nothing when the concept in the pinned release is not eligible as
a whole, or when the concept is retired, and the reading says why. Restoring a concept that is in a release is
refused.

**B6. Every statement of a decision-bearing form rests on its own quote.**
- Each entry of a sub-list (`dependsOn`, `doesNotEstablish`, `inputs`, `steps`, `outputs` and the like) carries a
  quote inside the item's lines, or is keyed to one.
- A `quotes[]` entry names the field it grounds, and quotes are distinct after whitespace is collapsed.
- The number rule reads each text against its own quote, not against the pool, and also reads the claim's own
  statement, conditions and limitations.
- Teaching forms keep one quote per object.
- This changes the extraction shape of design Appendix A.2. Record it as a deviation; S4 follows the code.

**B7. A concept id is not a design record's reference.** An id with the shape of a record reference (the prefixes of
the record graph's object types, such as `REQ-001`, `IF-001`, `ADR-001`) is refused. Identifier fields hold
identifiers (must-fix 2); `calculation.modelKind` is an identifier.

**B8. A limit must be reachable through the commands.**
- The history bound is part of the tier. Set the library tier's bound so that one tranche (60 concepts, nine forms
  each) with two correction rounds fits. History is never trimmed.
- A case writes a tranche through the commands, not by editing the document, and reports the characters used against
  the 8,000,000-character bound. If a tranche of 60 does not fit, say what does and set the tier to that.
- The literals in `knowledge-service.js`, `repository-transfer.js` and `repository-packet.js` read the tier.

**B9. An opposing candidate.** With B3 a non-person cannot add one to a reviewed concept. A person's opposing
candidate keeps today's fail-safe behaviour; record it as a known limit at handbook scale.

**B10. For later slices; record under "next" in the notes.**
- S3: the reading takes a released concept's identity from the release parts, not from the register; a currency state
  and a finding for a concept retired behind an active pin; `KINDS` extended (about 60 B) before S4; `designKeys` and
  `evidenceKeys` for `knowledge.concepts`, `.topics` and `.library` through S0.
- S4: the import script always states author kind `import`; the review pack says that the third-person rule rests on
  the author kind a request states.

## D. Shared registration (S0) after its adversarial review

The review of S0 (verdict: changes needed) found five faults that must be fixed and thirteen that should be. All
eighteen are to be done as the reviewer states them, with the rulings below. The review is on file for the engineers
at `/home/user/hb-review/rc44-build/s0-extensions.review.json`.

**D1. A second person's step needs two persons** (must-fix 1; this is how plan ruling 5 is to be read).
- After `verifierRefusal`, the boundary refuses unless a person other than the actor stands behind the record as it
  stands: its author stamp is a person's, or a person has confirmed or kept it. One person accepting what only an
  agent or a draft by Sol wrote is one person, not two.
- A record with no author stamp has no person behind it, and the step is refused (see D12).
- A package that states a `second` type states `provenance.paths` for the collection it acts on; the start check
  refuses one that does not.
- The refusal says in words that fit any package what must happen first: a second person saves or confirms the record.

**D2. A document says which packages have written into it** (must-fix 2).
- The boundary writes the package's id under one key of the document when a command or an upgrade step of that package
  first writes. Neither fingerprint reads the key. A document no package wrote into does not gain it. The contract
  names the key.
- Server, for a document that names a package this build does not register: reading the project works and names the
  missing package; every command, and every route that assembles a document for export (registers, SDD, ARS), is
  refused in words. A design that this build cannot read in full is not changed, frozen or printed by it.
- Client: `ensureExtensions` rejects, naming the package, when the document names one that has no route stub. A load
  that fails after the server answered "saved" is reported as saved and not yet shown; the store goes to `behind`, not
  to `error`, and never says another tab changed the project.

**D3. One statement of the keys the workbench itself writes** (must-fix 3), on the server, held by a suite case against
every key of every template and of documents after the built-in command families. An evidence key, a lifecycle path
or a `provenance.records` path on such a key is refused at start. A new sub-key of a chapter container stays allowed.

**D4. A package may state a tracker for every commit** (must-fix 4): `track(prior, next, command, at)` at package
level, called after the built-in trackers for every command and for each inner command of a batch. It writes nothing
for a document without the package's records. What it writes is part of the same commit and is journalled. The
per-type `track` stays or is folded in; the contract describes one way only.

**D5. The SDD's reading of who wrote the records reads the packages' collections too** (must-fix 5), in S0: the counts
and the "Drafted by Sol, to keep, change or retire" items. A document without such records prints as before.

**D6. The should-fix items.** All thirteen are to be done. Where one needs a ruling:
- *1.* The undo of a person-only or second-person step is itself a person's step: an agent account, a draft by Sol, an
  import and a request with no signed-in actor are refused. It stays with the account that saved the step.
- *2.* `validate` is asked for every command an alternative carries: when it is saved, when it is revised and when it
  is merged.
- *3 and 8.* With none of a package's records in a document, no trail of that document moves. A hop onto a layer of a
  built-in type states `only`; the start check refuses one that does not. The walk skips a package's layer whose type
  no object of the document has, if that fits the allowance of D9; if not, it is recorded under "next" with its cost.
- *4.* The contract states both ways a package could put a record on a built-in trail (a relation, and `add` with a
  built-in type). S0's shared reading helper for package suites asserts that the built-in layers and every spine are
  unchanged with the package's records present; every package's suite calls it.
- *10.* Within the allowance of D9 if it fits; if not, under "next" with its cost.
- *11.* Fix it, or refuse a single-segment lifecycle path at start: whichever costs no route bytes.

**D7. Answers the contract must give** (should-fix 12).
1. `second` answers the record, or a projection `{id, actor, history, owner?}` the package builds. `actor` and
   `history` name everyone who wrote what the step covers. A projection may leave out saves that did not change what

   a save left out. At read time a package calls `verifierRefusal` on the same projection with the stored step's actor

2. `add` may emit a record of a built-in type only when that type stands on no built-in layer (today `pattern`), and

3. Say what `selected` does. `created` is either read by something or removed from the contract.
4. Say exactly where a list-11 finding shows and where it does not, as it is for the built-in ones.
5. A type that ends in `.review`, `.handoff` or `.intake` is a review command to `authorship.js`. A package's type with
   such an ending states `person` or `second`; the start check refuses one that does not.
6. A package is held, from its first slice, in the lazy entry its design names for its surface (wp2: the chunk rooted
   at `public/architecture-task-ui.js`). The lead confirms the host entry at each package's first slice.
7. Add wp2's "styles and patterns" list of the SDD to "What the registry does not carry".

## E. Knowledge forms (wp1 S1) after its fix-up

The fix-up did every item of section B and found one more gap, which it closed (a plain claim could open a

**E1. No request of a non-person lowers the standing of a reviewed or released concept** (extends B3).
The fix-up measured one path that is still open: `knowledge.source` with `supersedes` makes every claim on the
superseded source ineligible, whoever sends it. An agent account or an import can so make a reviewed concept read
"draft" and then edit it, or switch off a released concept (a pin then hands over nothing).
- In a library project, a request written by an agent account, as a draft by Sol, as an import, or stating no author is
  refused when it would make a claim of a concept ineligible, lapse a concept's review, or retire or withdraw anything
  a concept rests on, where the concept's review stands or the concept is in a release. Known paths: a superseding
  source, an opened contradiction that names a form claim, an opposing claim (closed by the fix-up).
- A person's request keeps today's fail-safe effect.
- An import still brings a changed rendition as a new source; a person sends the supersession (S4 plans for it).
- The proof is a walk, not a list: every `knowledge.*` command type, sent by each kind of non-person, against a
  reviewed concept and against a released, active one, leaves the concept's standing and what a pin hands over as they
  were. A command type added later joins the walk or the case fails.

**E2. The Brain's receipt does not name a release of forms** (the fix-up's own decision under B1): confirmed. The
receipt lists what the Brain read, and it reads no form. B1 has used 65 B of its 120 B.

**E3. A library holds one tranche** (completes B8). Measured through the commands: about 100,000 characters for each
concept with its first release and about 47,000 more for each successor release of the whole tranche, against a bound

- The library tier's concept bound is 60, not 300: a bound of 300 cannot be reached.
- The character bound may still bind first. Its refusal says what to do: start another library for the next tranche.
- S4 plans one library project for each tranche, and sizes a tranche by these figures.
- Releases stay whole, immutable copies, as on rc.10.73.42.

**E4. A release holds plain claims or forms, never both** (the fix-up's reading of B1): confirmed. S3 follows the code,
not design section c.

**E5. The concept register is part of the built-in knowledge family.** A document that holds concepts names no package
for them (ruling D2 is about packages). `p10-knowledge` is what S3 registers: the pin, the `concept` type, its
finding and its SDD section. A design document names `p10-knowledge` when a pin is first written into it.

**E6. For S3, before a pin is read interactively:** reading a tranche-sized active release digests the whole release
once for each claim. S3 settles it inside wp1's allowance (the fix-up estimates 70 B).

**E7. Known limits that stay** (recorded, not closed): a person's opposing claim makes a reviewed concept read "draft"
