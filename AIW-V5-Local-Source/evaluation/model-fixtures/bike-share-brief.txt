Neighbourhood bike share — project brief (AIW authored teaching example; no real organisation or figures)

Context
A town council wants a small bike-share service for its three neighbourhoods. Residents pick up a bike at a dock, ride, and return it to any dock. The council's transport team owns the service; a maintenance contractor services the bikes; the council's finance office collects the fees.

Goals
The goal is to cut short car trips within the town by making a bike available within a five-minute walk of most homes. We want residents to be able to join, unlock and pay without visiting an office, so that the service runs with a small team.

Users and stakeholders
- Residents who ride (members and occasional riders).
- The transport team, who run the service day to day.
- The maintenance contractor, who repairs and rebalances bikes.
- The finance office, which reconciles payments.
- The council's data protection officer, accountable for personal data.

In scope
- Membership sign-up and identity checks for adults.
- Unlocking a bike at a dock with the mobile app or a member card.
- Trip recording, pricing and payment.
- Dock and bike status for the transport team and the contractor.
Out of scope
- E-scooters and cargo bikes.
- Integration with the regional bus ticket.

Journey
1. A resident signs up in the app and confirms their email.
2. The resident finds a dock with an available bike on the map.
3. The resident unlocks a bike with the app or a card.
4. The trip is recorded while the bike is away from a dock.
5. The resident returns the bike to a dock, and the trip ends.
6. The fee is calculated and charged to the saved payment method.
7. The transport team sees docks that are full or empty and asks the contractor to rebalance.

Requirements
- The service must unlock a bike within three seconds of a valid request at the dock.
- The app must show available bikes per dock, refreshed at least every thirty seconds.
- A trip must be recorded exactly once, even if the dock loses its connection and reconnects.
- The service must charge the correct fee for every completed trip and never charge twice.
- Members must be able to see their trip history and receipts.
- The transport team must be able to mark a bike as out of service and see which docks are empty or full.
- The contractor should receive a rebalancing list each morning.
- The finance office should be able to export a daily reconciliation of charges.
- The service should keep working for unlocking when the central system is unreachable for up to ten minutes.

Constraints
- Personal data must be handled under GDPR; trip locations must not be kept longer than ninety days.
- Payments must use the council's existing payment provider and must not store card numbers.
- The first neighbourhood must go live before the start of the summer season.
- The dock hardware is already chosen and speaks a vendor protocol over mobile data.

Assumptions
- We assume docks have mobile coverage at every site.
- We assume the payment provider offers a tokenised card on file.
- We expect fewer than two thousand trips a day in the first year.

Open questions
- Who is accountable for incidents out of office hours?
- What is the target for the number of trips per bike per day?
